# Plan: next — the shipped role set cannot publish an offer or observe a merge (os-d6a52784)

**Three** capabilities the ordinary contract lifecycle requires are
granted by nothing in `next/lanes/`. The card named two; the third came
out of review (#210) and is the worst of them:

- `offer.published` accepts `[supervise, operator]`. Nothing grants
  `supervise`.
- `merge.observed` accepts `[observer, operator]`. Nothing grants
  `observer`.
- `check.sealed` accepts **`[sealer]` alone** — no operator row at all
  (`keyring.go`: "Like the verdict lane it has no operator fallback").
  Nothing grants `sealer`, and above `trivial` the sealed-checks lint is
  not exempt, so sealing is on the ordinary path for every contract the
  tier system does not wave through.

The third is worse than the two because `operator` cannot paper over it.
For offers and merges a deployment can limp along by making the
maintenance lane do routing and forge observation, which is wrong but
possible. For sealed checks there is no such escape hatch: the
capability is disjoint from `claim` and from `operator` at
`actor.granted` admission, so **no shipped key can author a sealed check
at all**.

The six shipped manifests grant: curator none, dispatcher `dispatch`,
implementer `claim`, maintenance `maintenance` + `operator`, planner
`claim`, verifier `verdict`.

So a deployment assembled from `next/lanes/` alone can neither publish
the offer its own workers poll for nor record the merge that ends the
loop. Both are steps in the ordinary path: offers are how work is
invited (`offers.md`), and `merge.observed` is the single transition to
`done` (`reconciliation.md`). Only the maintenance lane reaches either,
through `operator`, which is not its job — that lane's whole posture is
that it holds no private powers and performs defined maintenance acts,
not routing and not forge observation.

Found while implementing os-6a08b166 and deliberately not taken there:
that card's file scope declared `next/lanes/**` read-only. Its fixtures
stage a supervisor and an observer as **background identities outside
the mode's identity plan**, precisely so its grants assertion kept
measuring only lane-derived identities. Honest for a fixture; wrong as a
shipped posture.

## The card's premise is wrong, and the charter is why

The card's open question 1 asks "two new lanes, or two grants added to
existing ones?" **Neither.** Both branches are refused by the charter,
which is design authority and is not editable:

- **§II.11 is a closed enumeration.** "Six lanes; each a role (grants +
  conventions)", then dispatcher, planner, implementer, verifier,
  curator, maintenance, numbered 1 through 6. The glossary repeats the
  list. `next/spec/lanes.md` opens by citing it: "The charter's six
  (§II.11) are …". III.J's own row reads "all **six** lanes". Adding a
  seventh and eighth lane edits a normative enumeration by implication.
- **And the two roles already exist in the charter, outside that
  enumeration.** The supervisor is **§II.9, its own section** — "The
  supervisor **publishes offers**". The observer is §8's **"governed
  observers"**, the parties by whom `merge.observed` and `check.observed`
  enter the ledger. They are not missing lanes. They are roles the
  charter deliberately did not put in the work loop, because neither
  takes work: a supervisor invites it and an observer attests to what an
  external system did.

Adding `supervise` to the dispatcher would be the same error wearing a
smaller hat. `offers.md` calls an offer "**the supervisor's invitation**"
and the dispatcher's own manifest summary says it "runs with least
standing capability because it reads the most untrusted text" — handing
the tree's most persuadable actor the power to invite arbitrary workers
onto arbitrary contracts is precisely backwards.

So the card's question 2 answers itself: the capability tables are
right, and the **role set** shipped as `next/lanes/` is what is
incomplete.

## Design decisions (binding for this task)

- **D1 — two ROLE definitions, in the same directory and machinery,
  explicitly NOT lanes.** `next/lanes/supervisor.json` and
  `next/lanes/observer.json` ship with the six, validated by the same
  `internal/lane` code, composed from the same fragments. Each manifest
  gains a field saying which it is, and `lanes.md` gains a section
  saying the directory holds the charter's six work-loop lanes **plus**
  the charter's two non-loop roles, citing §II.9 and §8.

  The alternative — a second directory — is refused: it would duplicate
  the validation, the fragment composition and the drills for two files,
  and the thing that makes a manifest trustworthy is that one validator
  checks every one of them.

- **D2 — the distinguishing field is `kind`, with values `lane` and
  `role`, required on every manifest.** Not an `is_lane` boolean and not
  inferred from the name.

  Required rather than defaulted, because a defaulted field means the
  six existing manifests keep passing unchanged while silently acquiring
  a claim nobody wrote. Making it required means the six are edited to
  say `"kind": "lane"` in this PR, which is the point: the enumeration
  becomes a property of the files rather than of a sentence in
  `lanes.md` that can drift from them.

- **D3 — `internal/lane` validates the enumeration against the charter's
  six BY NAME.** Exactly six manifests may carry `"kind": "lane"`, and
  their names must be exactly dispatcher, planner, implementer,
  verifier, curator, maintenance.

  This is the load-bearing decision. Without it, D1 makes it easy to add
  a seventh lane by dropping a file in a directory — the charter's
  closed enumeration would be enforced by nothing at all. With it, the
  next agent that tries gets a refusal naming §II.11. A `role` manifest
  is unconstrained in name, because §II.9 and §8 enumerate nothing.

- **D4 — neither role declares `acts_through`, and the validator must
  not require it of them.** `internal/lane` obliges a manifest holding
  `CapClaim` to declare its acts. Neither role holds `claim`: a
  supervisor publishes and an observer attests, and neither takes work.
  The existing rule is already conditioned on `CapClaim` rather than on
  lane-ness, so it needs no change — which is worth confirming by drill
  rather than by reading, because "it happens to pass" and "it is
  correct" are different claims (`memory/LEARNINGS.md`).

- **D5 — the curator's empty grants set stays empty** (the card's
  question 3). It is the one role with no write path at all, deliberately:
  §II.12 has it "propose everything, approve nothing", through a
  grant-gated pipeline that does not exist until Phase 11. An empty
  grants set is the honest encoding of that and is not the same defect
  as a capability no role grants.

- **D5b — `sealer` is added to the VERIFIER manifest, not given a role
  of its own** (review finding on #210). The charter's requirement is
  narrow and specific: sealed checks are "authored under a grant
  disjoint from **implementation** grants" (§7). `verdict` is not an
  implementation grant, and `sealed-checks.md`'s own disjointness rule
  spells out what is forbidden — "sealer cannot join a key holding claim
  or operator" — which permits `verdict` by omission rather than by
  accident.

  The positive argument is stronger than the permission. The check
  bodies are encrypted **to the verifier keyring**, so the verifier is
  already the only party that can read them; a separate authoring
  identity would be a role that cannot read back what it wrote, and
  rotation of the verifier keyring would silently change who can audit
  its own past output. Nothing is concentrated that was not already
  concentrated: a compromised verifier key already decrypts every
  sealed check.

  What this forecloses, deliberately: `sealer` cannot go on implementer
  or planner (both hold `claim`) or maintenance (holds `operator`) —
  the grant rule refuses all three at `actor.granted`. And it must not
  go on the dispatcher, for the reason `supervise` must not: that lane
  reads the most untrusted text, and authoring the hidden checks is the
  last authority to hand it.

- **D6 — the drill whose absence let this reach Phase 9 is the real
  deliverable.** Every capability in `keyring.AcceptedCapabilities` for
  every verb on the ordinary contract path is granted by some shipped
  manifest, or the drill fails naming the verb and the capability.

  It is derived from the capability table rather than from a list, for
  the reason this card exists: a hand-listed drill cannot notice a verb
  it was never told about, and that is exactly how two capabilities went
  ungranted through an entire phase. `operator` is EXCLUDED from
  satisfying the check — it satisfies everything by construction, so
  counting it would let the maintenance lane paper over every future
  gap, which is the shape of the bug being fixed rather than the fix.

- **D7 — `kind` governs identity-plan construction in the mode
  fixtures, and `modes_e2e_test.go` is IN SCOPE** (review finding on
  #210). `fleetPlan` currently does `for _, m := range mustLoad(t)` and
  makes an identity per manifest, so dropping two JSON files into
  `next/lanes/` would provision supervisor and observer as fleet lanes
  seven and eight — the exact thing D1 says they are not. The plan that
  declared them non-lanes and left that loop alone would have
  contradicted itself in the tree.

  So the fleet plan filters on `kind == "lane"`, and the roles are
  provisioned from the **role manifests** rather than from the
  hard-coded background grants os-6a08b166 staged. That is what actually
  closes the posture gap: the fixtures stop staging supervisor and
  observer as identities invented by the test and start deriving them
  from the shipped role set, which is the claim this card exists to make
  true. A drill asserts the fleet has exactly six lane identities and
  that the role identities came from manifests.

- **D8 — scope guard.** No capability table changes, no transition row
  moves, no verb gains or loses an accepted capability. The tables were
  right; the role set was short. Nothing under `next/internal/keyring/**`
  or `next/spec/transitions.json` is touched.

  `sealer` joining the verifier manifest is a change to a **lane's
  grants**, not to a capability's accepted set: `check.sealed` still
  accepts `[sealer]` and nothing else.

## Steps

1. `next/lanes/supervisor.json`, `next/lanes/observer.json` — the two
   role manifests, with their summaries, grants (`supervise`,
   `observer`), orienting read, and empty acts (D1, D4).
2. `next/lanes/fragments/lane/supervisor.md`, `observer.md` — the prose
   fragments, composed from the same `common/` set the six use.
3. `next/lanes/*.json` — `"kind": "lane"` added to the six (D2).
4. `next/internal/lane/lane.go` — `kind` required and validated; the
   six-by-name check (D3); drills for each, including a seventh `lane`
   manifest refused by name and a `role` manifest accepted.
5. `next/lanes/verifier.json` — `sealer` added to its grants (D5b).
5b. `next/internal/lane/lane_test.go` or `next/cmd/seed/lane_cli_test.go`
   — the coverage drill (D6), derived from the capability table.
5c. `next/cmd/seed/modes_e2e_test.go` — `fleetPlan` filters on
   `kind == "lane"`, and the supervisor/observer identities come from
   the role manifests instead of the hard-coded background grants
   (D7).
6. `next/spec/lanes.md` — the section distinguishing the charter's six
   work-loop lanes from its two non-loop roles, citing §II.9 and §8; and
   the residual/gap note from os-6a08b166 updated, since the gap it

7. `next/docs/progress.md` — the os-d6a52784 gap recorded under Phase 9
   item 4 is closed, with what replaced it.
8. `next/docs/decisions.md`, `memory/LEARNINGS.md`; receipt; evidence;
   review.

## Acceptance Criteria

1. `supervise`, `observer` and `sealer` are each granted by exactly one
   shipped manifest, and `seed lane` (or the equivalent read) reports
   the two roles alongside the six lanes.
2. **The coverage drill fails on `main`'s tree.** Run against the
   pre-fix `next/lanes/`, it names all THREE gaps —
   `offer.published`/`supervise`, `merge.observed`/`observer` and
   `check.sealed`/`sealer`. A drill that has never seen the gap it
   exists to catch is a claim, not a check; and the count is three
   because the drill reads the capability table, which is the whole
   argument for deriving it rather than listing it. (The card named two.
   The third was found by review, not by the card, which is the same
   lesson one level up.)
3. `operator` does not satisfy the coverage check for any verb, drilled
   by removing `supervise` from the supervisor manifest and confirming
   the drill still fails — the maintenance lane's `operator` must not
   paper over it.
4. Exactly six manifests carry `"kind": "lane"`, and a seventh is
   refused **by name** with a message citing §II.11. A `"kind": "role"`
   manifest with any name is accepted.
5. `kind` is required: a manifest omitting it is refused rather than
   defaulted, drilled against a copy of a shipped manifest with the
   field removed.
6. Neither role is required to declare `acts_through`, and the
   implementer and planner still are — drilled in both directions, so
   the rule is shown to turn on `CapClaim` rather than on `kind`.
7. The fleet identity plan has **exactly six** lane identities after two
   role manifests are added, and its supervisor and observer identities
   are derived from those manifests rather than staged by the test
   (D7). Drilled by asserting the count and the provenance, so adding a
   role file cannot silently grow the fleet.
8. **Mutation evidence.** Each must fail a drill: the six-by-name check
   accepting a seventh lane; `kind` defaulted instead of required; the
   coverage drill counting `operator`; the coverage drill reading a
   hand-listed set of verbs instead of the capability table;
   `acts_through` required of a role; and `fleetPlan` iterating every
   manifest instead of filtering on `kind`.
9. `make check` green with coverage measured **cold**, at least three
   readings above the gate, and the suites pass **unprivileged** under
   `setpriv --reuid=65534`.
