# Plan: next — require-approval per verb on the machine surface (os-5781a026)

Charter §II.14 defines per-verb policy on the machine-protocol
surface as "allow/deny/require-approval by actor and risk class;
approvals as request events resolved attributably in an operator
inbox", and III.L row 4 asks that this policy govern the surface with
attributable approvals. Review on #320 (chatgpt-codex-connector) read
the definition back against the tree: allow and deny exist (the grant
table, the declaration's ceiling and routing rules, drilled on `serve`
by os-8ecef90f) and require-approval does not, so os-8ecef90f moved the
row to `partial` and routed its third mode here. This card builds the
mode and flips the row on all three together. Tier: standard (three
verbs, a declaration block, an admission rule, an inbox row). Deps:
none on `main`; os-8ecef90f's drills are the allow and deny halves the
row's evidence also cites.

## What the tree actually shows

- **Allow and deny, per verb.** `keyring.AcceptedCapabilities` admits
  each governed verb from a capability set and refuses the rest
  (`out_of_grant`); `policyRules` in `internal/admit` refuse
  `claim.taken` above a squad's agent ceiling and `intent.filed` to an
  undeclared squad, reading `Context.Declaration`. Both are policy
  rather than chain validity, no-ops without a declaration.
- **The inbox exists.** An escalation is a request a human answers:
  `escalation.raised` surfaces as `escalation.pending` owed by
  `lane:operator` in the obligations projection and the situation
  read, and `decision.recorded` (operator only) resolves it
  attributably. A request from outside the trust boundary surfaces the
  same way as `request.pending` owed by `lane:dispatch`. Nothing binds
  either to a verb an actor is waiting to perform.
- **Catalog growth is additive.** A new fact verb older validators
  refuse as unknown does not bump the protocol version
  (`protocol.md`, the `offer.*` precedent); a new optional field on an
  existing verb's strict payload would, since `escalation.raised`'s
  shape is decoded strictly. So the approval is its own verb family,
  not a field on the escalation.
- **The fold consumes nothing.** No fact today is spent by a later
  act; the closest shape is an offer, consumed by the claim that takes
  it (`LastClaim`).

## Design decisions (binding for this task)

- **D1 — the policy is declared by verb, actor, actor kind and tier
  floor.** `guardrails.approvals` in the declaration is a list of
  `{"verb": "<catalog verb>", "actors": ["<fingerprint>"], "kinds":
  ["agent", "service"], "min_tier": "<tier>"}`: an act of `verb` by a
  key the entry reaches on a contract whose tier is at or above
  `min_tier` (absent means every tier; a `system` subject has no tier
  and is governed whenever the verb is; a birth's tier is its filing's)
  requires an open approval. An entry reaches the keys `actors` names
  and the keys whose roster kind `kinds` names, and every non-human
  kind when it names neither, so the policy varies by actor (the
  charter's individual keypair: one agent allowed while another of the
  same kind needs an approval) and by risk class (the kind and the
  tier). A key with no asserted kind (a governance root, enrolled by
  genesis) is reached by no kind selector, only by its fingerprint.
  `posture.Config.ApprovalRule(verb)` reads it; `seed preseed check`
  holds each entry to the affordance catalog's verbs, fingerprint
  shape, the roster kinds and the tier vocabulary
  (`preseed_incomplete`); a floor outside the vocabulary fails closed
  at admission, the ceiling's posture. The three approval verbs
  themselves are never governed. Policy, not chain validity: no
  declaration, no rule.
- **D2 — three fact verbs, additive catalog growth from `seed/1`.**
  `approval.requested` `{"verb", "actor", "reason"}` on the contract
  the act concerns (or `system`): the actor named is the key that will
  act, the requester is whoever files it (the actor itself in the
  loop's case, or a lane on its behalf), active standing only, like
  `request.filed`, since asking grants nothing; `approval.granted`
  `{"request": "<position>"}` and `approval.denied` `{"request",
  "reason"}`, operator only, the fifth no-fallback row's posture
  (`decision.recorded`'s), citing an `approval.requested` on the same
  subject not yet answered. Strict shapes; the verb named must be a
  catalog verb; the reason one bounded line.
- **D3 — the fold keeps the facts and spends the grants.**
  `ApprovalFact{Pos, TS, Requester, Subject, Verb, Actor, Reason,
  Answered *int, Granted bool, Answerer, ConsumedAt *int}` at the fold
  level with `Approvals()`, `ApprovalAt(pos)` and
  `OpenApproval(subject, verb, actor)` (granted and not yet consumed,
  the oldest). A grant is consumed by the first record on its subject
  whose verb and actor it names, at that record's position, so one
  approval admits one act; a second act needs a second request. An
  unanswered or denied request admits nothing.
- **D4 — the admission rule.** `require-approval` in `policyRules`:
  for a record whose verb the declaration governs, whose actor the
  entry reaches, on a subject at or above the floor, refuse
  `approval_required` (exit 3, the policy family beside
  `tier_above_ceiling`) unless a VALID open grant stands for
  `(subject, verb, actor)`, the message naming the exact request to
  file (`seed approval request --subject <s> --verb <v>`) and, once one
  stands, the grant that would answer it. Valid is the laundering
  countermeasure `memory/LEARNINGS.md` fixes the shape of (the
  `RunStartValid` posture): the tolerant fold records any well-shaped
  raw push, so the rule re-judges each open grant position-accurately
  before trusting it, replaying the keyring to the grant's position to
  hold its signer to active operator standing and re-parsing the
  request it cites (a catalog verb, an enrolled actor, the same
  subject); a grant that fails either admits nothing and the act
  refuses as if none stood. The shapes and citations of the three verbs
  are held by a core rule (`approval`) regardless of declaration
  (`approval_refused`, exit 3), so a raw-pushed grant citing nothing
  folds as an anomaly and admits nothing. Kind is the keyring's at the
  position, as the ceiling rule reads it.
- **D5 — the inbox row.** `approval.pending` in the obligations
  projection, owed by `lane:operator`, one row per open request
  carrying its position and timestamp, discharged by
  `approval.granted` or `approval.denied`; the situation read carries
  it as every other obligation, so the request lands in the inbox the
  operator orients from and the row's `discharged_by` is held to the
  dischargers the sweep asserts.
- **D6 — the loop verbs derive what they can.** `seed approval
  request --subject <s> --verb <v> [--for <fingerprint>] --reason
  <text>` (the actor defaults to the requesting key), `seed approval
  grant --subject <s> [--request <position>]` and `seed approval deny
  --subject <s> [--request <position>] --reason <text>` (the request
  defaults to the oldest open one on the subject, refused as ambiguous
  when several stand), all in the shared transport shape; the group
  `approval` joins the registry, so `serve` carries the three as
  methods.
- **D7 — the affordances draft the three where they are legal.** The
  request probe names the probing key as the actor and `claim.taken`
  as the verb; the grant and deny probes cite the oldest open request
  on the subject, a zero position that the rule refuses when none
  stands. A governed act is listed for its actor exactly when an open
  grant stands, which the soundness class holds.
- **D8 — the machine-surface drill closes the row.** Through `serve`:
  an agent's governed act refuses `approval_required` naming the
  request; the agent's `approval.request` lands and surfaces as
  `approval.pending` owed by the operator; a claim-granted key's
  `approval.grant` refuses `out_of_grant`; the operator's grant lands
  and reads back with the operator's fingerprint as `actor`; the act
  admits and consumes the grant; the same act refuses again; a denied
  request admits nothing; the CLI gives the same codes for the same
  argv. III.L row 4 flips to `met`, evidence naming this drill, the
  boundary drills and os-8ecef90f's allow and deny drills, note naming
  the three modes; `conformance.md` regenerated.
- **D9 — bounds.** No protocol version bump, no transition-table row,
  no change to `escalation.*` or `request.*`, no change to the hook or
  the service beyond running the rule set. The policy governs the
  protocol's verbs, the acts a key signs into the ledger, which is
  what "by actor" can mean: `serve` authenticates nobody, and its
  methods that append no record (`docs.generate`, `protections.apply`,
  `boundary.fetch`) run with the caller's own local credentials
  outside the trust boundary and carry no actor, so a policy at
  method dispatch would govern nobody attributably; they stay
  ungoverned, the reading os-8ecef90f's drills record in
  `platform.md`, and the row's evidence names the three modes on the
  verbs. NOT `.seed/**`.

## Acceptance Criteria

**Boundary set (new, shown working):**

1. **The policy governs at the boundary.** Under a declaration naming
   a verb, a governed act by a named kind or a named fingerprint at or
   above the floor refuses `approval_required` naming the request;
   with no declaration, an unnamed kind, a fingerprint the entry does
   not name, or a contract below the floor, the act admits as today;
   an entry naming one fingerprint reaches that key and no other of
   its kind.
2. **An approval is a request resolved attributably and spent once.**
   The request surfaces as `approval.pending` owed by the operator
   lane; only an operator's grant admits the act; the grant is a
   signed record read back with the operator's fingerprint; the
   admitted act consumes it and the next act refuses again; a denial
   admits nothing; a grant citing no open request refuses; a
   raw-pushed grant signed by a key without operator standing at its
   position folds as a fact and admits nothing.
3. **The machine surface runs the whole path** (D8) with the CLI's
   own codes, and the affordances list a governed act for its actor
   exactly when an open grant stands.
4. **The declaration is linted.** `seed preseed check` refuses an
   approvals entry naming a verb outside the catalog, an approval
   verb, an actor that is no fingerprint, a kind outside the roster
   vocabulary or a tier outside the tier vocabulary.
5. **The row reads met** on all three modes; `seed docs check` clean.
6. `make check` green; no model identifiers in any committed artifact.

**Retention set (existing, shown unharmed):**

- Every existing verb's rule and shape is unchanged; the escalation
  and request channels are byte-identical to `main`; the transition
  table and the protocol version register are untouched; the
  affordance soundness class, the keyring and catalog completeness
  drills and the obligation sweep pass with the verbs and the kind
  added to their lists.
