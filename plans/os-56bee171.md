# os-56bee171 — Phase 13 item 1: racing mode as the per-squad opt-in with first-verified settlement

Build plan Phase 13 item 1: racing mode as the per-squad opt-in with
first-verified settlement (III.F). Charter §II.6 ("verbs that take
exclusivity or spend reservation are not [offline-capable] — unless a
squad explicitly opts into racing mode (duplicate execution tolerated,
first *verified* success settles — a deliberate compute-for-latency
trade, declared per squad, never an accident of connectivity)"),
III.F row 7 ("Racing mode exists only as an explicit per-squad opt-in
with its compute cost stated; offline claiming is impossible by
construction"). The lifecycle spec records the build plan's binding
default that racing was deferred entirely; this card lifts it.

## What the tree actually shows

- **One claim per contract, settled at admission.** `claim.taken` is
  the table's one exclusive row; the lifecycle rule refuses a second
  claim on an `in_progress` subject with exit 2 `contention` naming
  the holder and the fence; the fence is the admitted position of the
  claim, cited by every deliberate exit and by every fact the holder
  or a prior claimant signs (`next/spec/lifecycle.md`, "Claims and
  fences"). The fold keeps one `Claim{Holder, Fence}` per subject,
  clears it when the state leaves `in_progress`, and remembers every
  prior claimant.
- **The squad is declared, and the declaration already carries
  per-squad policy.** `seed.json`'s `guardrails.squads.<name>` holds
  the default and agent-ceiling tiers (Phase 12 item 4), read by the
  boundary as policy rules over the declaration; `teams.squads` names
  the lanes a squad runs; a contract's `routing` names its squad.
- **Submissions, verdicts and the merge chain are singular by
  construction.** The fold keeps the one review-entering
  `submission.made`; `verdict.rendered` binds to it by position; a fail
  locks the submitter out until a new submission; `merge.requested`
  cites the pass verdict and `merge.observed` closes the chain
  (`verdicts.md`, `reconciliation.md`).
- **Budgets are fenced to the claim** (`budgets.md`): `budget.reserve`
  cites the fence, settle and release close it, exhaustion parks with
  a packet. **Offers** invite claims and grant nothing (`offers.md`).
  **Reaping** (`claim.reaped`) is the maintenance actor's act with a
  packet, naming the claim it kills.
- **The cooperative client refuses to draft an exclusive verb offline**
  (`modes.md`): exclusivity is granted at admission, and only the push
  round-trip orders rivals.

## Design decisions (binding for this task)

**D1. The opt-in is a declared block with its cost in words.**
`guardrails.squads.<name>.racing` is `{"racers": N, "cost": "<the
statement>"}`: `racers` an integer ≥ 2 (the most claims the squad
tolerates on one contract at once), `cost` a non-empty sentence the
operator writes (the compute-for-latency trade, stated where the
opt-in is). An absent block is no racing, the behavior the tree has
today; a block with `racers` below 2 or an empty `cost` refuses
`posture_invalid` (exit 13) at load and at `seed preseed check`,
refined `preseed_incomplete` where Phase 12 item 4 (#254) lands that
refinement of exit 13 before this card does (`envelope.md` carries it
from that merge; until then the family code alone). The
declaration is the only source: no verb, flag or environment opts a
contract into racing. Refused: a per-contract opt-in (the charter
says per squad); a default cost sentence.

**D2. A racing claim admits beside the holders, each with its own
fence, up to the cap.** Under a declaration whose squad races,
`claim.taken` on an `in_progress` subject admits while the subject
holds fewer active claims than `racers` and the claimant holds none
of them; the record is a claim like any other, exclusive in the
table's sense (granted at admission, never offline), and its fence is
its own admitted position. The fold keeps `Claims` (every active
claim, by fence) beside `Claim` (the first of them, so every reader
of the singular fact reads what it read before); `PriorClaimants`
grows as today. The fence rule reads "the active fences": a deliberate
exit cites the exiting racer's own fence and kills that claim alone;
a fact by a racer cites its own fence; a citation of another racer's
fence refuses `fenced_out` naming the fences. The subject stays
`in_progress` while any claim is active. Without the opt-in, or at
the cap, the second claim refuses `contention` exactly as today, the
envelope naming every holder and fence. Refused: a racing claim
without a declaration; a racer holding two claims on one contract.

**D3. First verified success settles.** A racer's `submission.made`
records that racer's submission (the fold keeps `Submissions`, by
fence, beside the singular `Submission`, which is the first) and
kills that racer's claim; the subject enters `review` at the first
submission and stays there while other racers work — their claims
are active on a `review` subject, which the fold now permits only for
racing subjects. `verdict.rendered` binds to the submission it cites
by position, so verdicts on different racers' submissions coexist; a
fail locks that submitter out as today and touches no other racer.
The first pass verdict admitted is the settlement: `merge.requested`
cites it, `merge.observed` closes the chain, and from that position
every other active claim on the subject is **settled-out**: the fold
marks them, a settled-out racer's next act refuses `race_settled`
(exit 3 refined, naming the settling verdict's position), its
packet-bearing exit still admits (a deliberate exit is never refused
after the fact), and the maintenance actor's reaper
(`seed maintain`) reaps what does not exit on its own, with a packet
naming the settlement. Refused: a second pass verdict settling anything
(the chain is closed); a tie (chain order is total).

**D4. Spend is per racer and the race is visible.** Every racer
reserves against its own fence; the budget view sums the subject's
open reservations; the report's `contracts` section shows
`racers`, `settled_by` and `settled_out` per subject and the squad's
race count and spend; the situation read tells a racer it is racing
(`racing: {racers, cost}`) at claim time, so the cost sentence is in
front of the lane that spends it. Refused: a shared reservation.

**D5. Offline claiming stays impossible by construction.** The
table's `exclusive` flag is untouched, the cooperative client keeps
refusing to draft `claim.taken` offline, and the racing judgment lives
in the lifecycle rule beside contention: two racers ordered by the
push round-trip, never by a clock.

**D6. Drills.** The boundary: two racers on a racing squad (both
admit, each fence its own), a third at the cap refusing `contention`
naming both holders, the same two claims on a non-racing squad
refusing as today, a racer citing the other's fence refusing
`fenced_out`, a racer's park killing its claim alone, both racers
submitting, one verdict failing (its submitter locked out, the other
untouched), the other passing and settling, the loser's next act
refusing `race_settled`, the loser's own park still admitting, the
reaper reaping a silent loser with a packet naming the settlement,
and every reservation settling to its own fence. The fold: the
singular facts unchanged on every non-racing chain (projections
byte-identical on the corpus). The declaration: the block's shape at
lint and load. The report and the situation read. The trajectory
corpus re-recorded only if a shipped lane's frame changes (it should
not: no shipped lane races). Mutation evidence: a racing claim
admitted without the opt-in; a third racer past the cap; a settlement
that does not settle out; a loser's exit refused; a shared fence.

**D7. Bounds.** Racing is a per-squad opt-in and nothing else: no
supervisor schedules racers (offers stay eligibility-scoped
invitations; a supervisor that wants a race publishes more than one
offer and says so in the cost sentence), and no lane ships racing.
The plan gate, the ceilings, the independence levels and the
red-verdict lockout apply per racer unchanged.

## Acceptance Criteria

**Boundary set (new, shown working):**

1. **The opt-in is declared.** A squad with a `racing` block races; a
   squad without one refuses the second claim exactly as today; a
   block with fewer than two racers or no cost sentence refuses at
   lint and load.
2. **Each racer holds its own fence.** Two racers admit up to the cap
   with distinct fences; a third refuses `contention` naming both; a
   racer citing another's fence refuses `fenced_out`; a racer's exit
   kills its claim alone.
3. **First verified success settles.** Submissions coexist by fence;
   verdicts bind to their submission; the first pass verdict settles;
   the loser's next act refuses `race_settled`, its own exit admits,
   and the reaper reaps a silent loser with a packet naming the
   settlement.
4. **Spend is per racer and visible.** Reservations settle to their
   own fences; the report and the situation read show the race and
   the cost sentence.
5. **Offline claiming is impossible.** The cooperative client refuses
   to draft a racing claim offline as it does any exclusive verb.
6. **Mutation evidence.** Each fails a drill: a racing claim without
   the opt-in; a third racer past the cap; a settlement that does not
   settle out; a loser's exit refused; a shared fence.
7. `make check` green with coverage measured cold, three readings
   above the gate; no model identifiers in any committed artifact.

**Retention set (existing, shown unharmed):**

- Every pre-existing chain verifies byte for byte; every projection
  is byte-identical on chains with no racing claim; the singular
  `Claim` and `Submission` facts read as before on every non-racing
  subject; `contention`, `fenced_out`, the red-verdict lockout, the
  plan gate and the ceilings are unchanged off the racing path; the
  trajectory corpus replays green.
