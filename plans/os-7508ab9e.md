# os-7508ab9e — Phase 12 item 3: checkpoint trust, the replay-equals-genesis proof, and performance budgets

Build plan Phase 12 item 3. Two charter rows, both III.A. Row 8's
mechanism landed with the maintenance loop (#205): `system.checkpoint`
carries `{format, snapshot, location, position}`, the snapshot is the
canonical projection state stored retrievably in the artifact store, and
a fresh reader fetches, verifies against the signed hash, and reads. What
the row still asks for is the **trust** half — "a fresh clone's
verification obligations (full replay once, or explicit trust in the
signer set) are documented and the choice is declared, not defaulted;
replay-from-checkpoint equals replay-from-genesis in CI". Row 12 asks
that "ledger performance is budgeted and tracked in CI: admission
latency, replay time, projection rebuild time, against a representative
history", and nothing under `next/` measures anything today.

## What the tree actually shows

- **The snapshot is the projection files at one verified position.**
  `checkpoint.Materialize(count, tip, files)` serializes
  `{format, position, tip, files}` with the files keyed
  `<projection>/<file>` from the same registered builders `project
  rebuild` publishes (`maintainSession.materialize`); the digest is the
  artifact key. `ReadSnapshot` refuses a format it does not know, which
  is the safe direction the spec names.
- **The reader exists only as a drill.**
  `TestMaintainCheckpointIsStartableByAFreshReader` re-reads the
  checkpoint from the chain, fetches and verifies the snapshot, and
  compares its files to a rebuild at the cited prefix. No verb does
  this, and nothing declares whether a deployment's readers may.
- **The hash does not cover the signature.** `Event.Hash` is SHA-256
  over the canonical event bytes; `Record` carries `sig` beside them.
  So a record whose signature never verified — or was altered after
  the fact — has the same hash as an honest one, and an attested tip
  cannot tell them apart. That is the exact content of "trust in the
  signer set": the reader trusts that admission verified what it
  admitted, which under an enforced posture is the boundary's promise
  and under cooperative is nobody's.
- **Who may checkpoint is already a capability row.**
  `system.checkpoint` accepts `maintenance` or `operator` (`actors.md`).
- **Nothing measures.** `grep -rn "func Benchmark" next` is empty; the
  coverage gate (`cmd/covergate`, `internal/covergate`) is the one
  place `make check-next` applies a numeric threshold, and its
  re-collect-cold-once rule is the shape a performance gate needs for
  the same reason: one noisy reading must not fail a healthy tree.
- **Three race drills exist, none tracked as a number.**
  `TestClaimRaceStorm` (100 appenders, `internal/gitref`),
  `TestRaceDrillTwoConcurrentAppenders`, `TestRemoteAppendRaceRetriesAndLands`
  — III.C row 4's "contention benchmark at hundreds of concurrent
  actors, tracked in CI" is the neighbouring row with no owner.

## Design decisions (binding for this task)

**D1. The trust choice is a declaration in `seed.json`, and an absent
declaration is undeclared, never a default.** `posture.Config` gains a strict
`checkpoints` block: `{"trust": "replay" | "signers"}`. `replay` says
every fresh reader verifies from genesis once before it serves anything;
`signers` says a fresh reader may start from the newest admitted
checkpoint whose signer held the `system.checkpoint` capability at the
checkpoint's own position, verifying only the records after it. An
absent block is not `replay` by default — it is undeclared, and a
reader asked to start from a checkpoint refuses with the refinement
`trust_undeclared` under exit 4 (a `not_found` whose subject is a config
field, the `posture_undeclared` shape) — because "the choice is
declared, not defaulted" is the row's own sentence. Three cases, then:
`replay`, `signers`, and undeclared — the third refusing to start from
a checkpoint and reporting the choice as unmade, which is not the
first case wearing a default. The block composes
with the `admission` (#244) and `protected` (#241) extensions of the
same declaration; whichever lands second merges the struct.

**D2. `seed project start` is the reader verb, and it is honest about
what it skips.** `seed project start --ledger <dir> --artifacts <dir>
--out <root> --config seed.json`: under `signers`, it parses the whole
chain (parsing is not verification), folds the keyring to judge the
checkpoint signer at its position, fetches the snapshot, verifies the
digest, checks `position` and `tip` against the chain at that prefix,
verifies linkage and signatures for the suffix only, rebuilds every
registered projection over the full record list, and asserts the
snapshot's files equal its own build at the cited position before
publishing anything — a checkpoint that lies about the state it
attests is refused as `checkpoint_mismatch` under exit 21 (the
`receipt_mismatch` family: a recomputation that fails to reproduce a
cited digest). What it skips is exactly the prefix's signature
verification and nothing else; the stamp of every build it publishes
carries `trust: "signers"` and the checkpoint position, so a consumer
can see the basis. Under `replay` it says so and exits 0 having done
nothing but name `project rebuild`. Refused: a reader that trusts the
snapshot's files without recomputing — the cross-check costs one fold
and turns a lying checkpoint from an invisible failure into a named one.

**D3. Replay-from-checkpoint equals replay-from-genesis, proven in CI
with teeth.** The drill builds a representative history (D5),
checkpoints it through the maintenance pass, appends more, and runs
`project start` under `signers` into one root and `project rebuild` into
another: every published build byte-identical, stamps aside. Its teeth:
a prefix record whose signature bytes are corrupted after the checkpoint
(same hash, same chain) is caught by `rebuild` and NOT by `start` — the
drill asserts both, because the second half is what the trust
declaration means and a proof that hid it would be the wrong proof.
A suffix record corrupted the same way is caught by both.

**D4. `next/spec/checkpoints.md` is the trust document.** What a
checkpoint attests (the `(position, tip)` pair and the projection state
under a versioned format), who may sign it, what a reader under each
declaration verifies and skips, the hash-excludes-signature fact and its
consequence, why a lying checkpoint under `signers` is bounded by the
signer set (the same keys that may halt admission), the staleness bound
III.A row 5 names (a fresh clone is at best as fresh as the newest
checkpoint or head attestation it can reach), and the format's
versioning rule. `maintenance.md`'s checkpoint section points here rather
than growing.

**D5. The representative history is generated, seeded and sized by
declaration.** `internal/history` (new): a deterministic generator that
drives N contracts through the fold-legal loop (file, specify, claim,
run bracket, submit, verdict, merge chain, done; a share parked, a
share escalated, budgets reserved and settled) with keys derived from a
fixed seed, so two machines produce the same chain, and the drills and
the benchmarks share it. The default size is declared in the budget
file, not in code.

**D6. Budgets are a checked-in file with headroom, applied by a gate
that re-measures cold once.** `next/perf/budgets.json`: for each
metric, the ceiling and the history size it was set against. Metrics:
admission latency (`admit.Check` at the tip of the history, per
proposal), replay time (`VerifyFromGenesis`), rebuild time (`RebuildWith`
over the registered set), and — taking III.C row 4 here, since a
contention number is a performance number and this is the phase whose
exit walks III.C — contention: a storm of hundreds of concurrent
appenders (the count declared) against a bare remote under the hook,
measured as wall time and as the attempts-per-append ratio, with no
lost update. `cmd/perfgate` + `internal/perfgate` mirror the coverage
gate: measure, compare to the ceiling, and on a miss re-measure cold
once before failing, so a noisy reading cannot fail a healthy tree
while a real regression fails twice. Ceilings are set at three times
the measured value on the CI runner class and the file says so; a
change that moves a number past its ceiling either fixes the
regression or raises the ceiling in the same PR with the reason in the
file — never silently. Refused: asserting exact numbers; a benchmark
that runs only by hand.

**D7. The numbers are visible.** `seed perf run` prints the measurements
as an envelope and `make check-next` runs the gate after coverage; the
last reading is written to `next/perf/last.json`, gitignored, so an
operator can read it and CI can attach it. Refused: committing readings,
which vary per machine.

**D8. Scope bounds.** No projection engine change beyond the stamp
field; no incremental fold (the reader folds everything and skips only
prefix signature verification, stated as such); no cache generation
bump unless the stamp field forces one, and the plan for that is "then
bump and say so". The contention benchmark measures the hook posture
only; the service posture's number is item 2's to add when it lands.

## Acceptance Criteria

**Boundary set (new, shown working):**

1. **The choice is declared, not defaulted.** `project start` refuses
   `trust_undeclared` with no block, does nothing but name `rebuild`
   under `replay`, and starts under `signers`; the doctor reports the
   declaration; an unknown value refuses `posture_invalid`.
2. **The reader verifies what it says it verifies.** Under `signers`
   the snapshot digest, `position` and `tip` are checked against the
   chain, the suffix's linkage and signatures are verified, the
   snapshot's files equal the reader's own build at the cited position
   (`checkpoint_mismatch` otherwise), only a checkpoint with a capable
   signer at its own position is used, and every published stamp
   carries the trust basis and the checkpoint position.
3. **Replay-from-checkpoint equals replay-from-genesis, with teeth
   (D3).** Byte-identical builds on the representative history; the
   prefix-signature corruption caught by `rebuild` and not by `start`;
   the suffix corruption caught by both.
4. **Performance is budgeted and tracked.** `seed perf run` measures
   the four metrics on the generated history at the declared size;
   `budgets.json` carries a ceiling and its provenance for each; the
   gate passes on `main`, fails on a planted regression (a sleep in the
   admission path in a drill), and re-measures cold once before
   failing; `make check-next` runs it.
5. **The storm at hundreds.** The contention benchmark runs the
   declared count of concurrent appenders against a hook-guarded bare
   remote with no lost update and reports wall time and
   attempts-per-append.
6. **The generator is deterministic**: two runs from one seed produce
   one chain, byte for byte.
7. **Mutation evidence.** Each fails a drill: the reader trusting the
   snapshot files without recomputing; skipping suffix signatures;
   accepting an incapable signer; defaulting an absent declaration to
   `signers`; the gate failing on the first noisy reading; a ceiling
   raised without provenance; the generator reading the clock.
8. `make check` green with coverage measured cold, three readings above
   the gate; the suites pass unprivileged; no model identifiers in any
   committed artifact.

**Retention set (existing, shown unharmed):**

- `project rebuild`, the maintenance pass and the checkpoint payload are
  unchanged: every existing checkpoint, projection and maintenance drill
  passes as before; every pre-existing chain verifies byte for byte.
- No new verb, transition row, capability or protocol version; the
  projections' builds are byte-identical on chains this card does not
  create, stamps aside where the new field applies.
- `make check`'s coverage gate and its re-collect rule are untouched;
  the perf gate runs after it and cannot mask it.
