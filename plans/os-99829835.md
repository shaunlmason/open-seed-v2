# Plan: next — Phase 10 item 3, independence levels L2/L3 declared per tier and recorded in verdicts (os-99829835)

The build plan's Phase 10 item 3: *"Independence levels L2/L3 declared
per tier and recorded in verdicts."* The charter's definition (§7,
"Independence is failure-domain separation"): *"L1 — distinct keys and
workspaces (minimum, always); L2 — distinct runtime tuples (different
model family or provider, different harness image); L3 — deterministic
verification first (tests, assertions, diffs) with model judgment only
in the residue, plus distinct evidence-acquisition paths. High-
consequence contracts require the level their tier declares, and the
verdict records which level was actually achieved."* Conformance III.G:
*"Independence levels L1–L3 are defined, declared per tier, enforced at
verdict time, and recorded in the verdict; high-consequence tiers
require runtime-tuple separation (L2) or deterministic-first
verification (L3)."*

This plan is written against the tier table of os-be12ac16
(`next/spec/tiers.md`, #222) and the eval machinery of os-03e47abb
(`next/spec/evals.md`, #221), and depends on both merging first.

## What the tree actually shows

Measured, not assumed:

- **`independence` is a literal.** `verdict.rendered`'s strict payload
  admits only `"L1"` (`admit.go`, the verdict rule; `verdicts.md`: "the
  level vocabulary widens when Phase 10 declares levels per tier"),
  `seed verdict render` hard-codes it, and the fold's `VerdictFact`
  does not record it at all. L1 is enforced today as key disjointness
  at admission (`not_independent`, exit 17) and workspace disjointness
  by construction (the verifier's clean per-run checkout).
- **The tier table has the column's slot and no column.** `tiers.md`
  declares plan required, sealed checks required and human review per
  tier, names item 3 as the owner of the `independence` column, and
  reads every gate through `TierGates`, an unknown tier taking the
  strictest row.
- **The implementer's configuration is a record; the verifier's is
  not.** `run.started` declares the worker's five-field tuple in the
  claim window (`qualification.md`), folded to `RunStartFact.Tuple`
  and re-judged by `RunStartValid`. Nothing declares what the verifier
  ran as: `verdict render` takes no tuple, and the receipt's
  `environment` fingerprint (`os`, `arch`, `go`, `runner`) names the
  machine, not the model or harness.
- **Deterministic-first is the pipeline's existing rule, unnamed.**
  `verdicts.md`: the render derives the permissible verdict from the
  transcripts it just executed, a red transcript forbids pass, the
  acceptance spec runs gated at an anchored revision in the verifier's
  own checkout, and `verdict check` recomputes everything. A prose-only
  spec leaves the verdict to the verifier's judgment. The qualitative
  residue (rubrics) is item 4.
- **The gate on the eval semantics is an equality.** `version.EvalApplies`
  is `v == seed/3` (os-03e47abb D8, deliberate while no later version
  existed); `memory/LEARNINGS.md` already records that an "activates at
  N" gate written as equality closes on N+1.
- **Both fixtures share one model.** The small-team and fleet drills
  render with a verifier key and no configuration; every contract they
  drive is `trivial` or `standard`.

## Design decisions (binding for this task)

- **D1 — the levels are ordered, achieved is the highest that holds,
  and the tier table declares the minimum.** `tiers.md` gains the
  `independence` column:

  | tier | independence |
  |---|---|
  | `trivial` | `L1` |
  | `standard` | `L1` |
  | `critical` | `L2` |

  `TierRow` gains `Independence` and the strictest row requires `L3`.
  Achieved is computed, never asserted: **L1** holds for every admitted
  verdict (the existing disjointness); **L2** holds when the verifier's
  declared tuple (D2) differs from the window's admitted declaration in
  model **provider or family**, or in harness name, the charter's
  "different model family or provider, different harness image", with
  principal, tool policy, environment and versions not counting. The
  `model` convention of `qualification.md` widens to an optional
  provider segment, `<provider>/<family>/<version>` beside
  `<family>/<version>` (review finding on the plan PR): a three-part
  model compares provider and family, a two-part one family alone, and
  two providers serving one family through one harness are L2. The
  tuple parser stays presence-only; the comparison is the level's.
  **L3** holds when the record proves deterministic-first verification
  on a distinct path (review finding on the plan PR: "executable and
  gated" proves only that a reviewed command ran): the contract's
  acceptance is executable and gated, the cited receipt carries at
  least one transcript, and **the receipt reproduces**: `verdict
  check`'s recomputation from the verifier's own checkout yields the
  cited digest, which is the record-backed determinism predicate (a
  nondeterministic command, a model call that answers differently, or
  an evidence path the implementer could shape yields a different
  transcript digest and fails to reproduce). Distinct acquisition is
  the workspace rule `verdicts.md` already makes structural (the
  verifier's own clone, objects copied). What the record cannot prove
  is stated: the `exec` profile declares the network rather than
  denying it, and a deterministic command that still reaches a model
  is caught only when the model disagrees with itself; the denying
  profile is Phase 13's adapter. The order is L1 < L2 < L3, and a
  tier's requirement is satisfied by any achieved level at or above
  it, which is III.G's "L2 or L3" for high-consequence tiers:
  deterministic-first verification is the stronger separation, since a
  reproduced transcript does not share a model with anyone. The verdict
  records the highest achieved.

  Refused: an unordered set of flags. The charter calls them levels and
  the conformance row says "L2 or L3" for one requirement, which is an
  ordering and nothing else. Refused: requiring L2 on `standard`. The
  ordinary contract must remain judgeable by a deployment with one
  model family (the small-team floor); `critical` is the charter's
  "high-consequence" tier and the one that pays for a second family or
  a deterministic spec.

- **D2 — the verifier declares its tuple at render, exactly as the
  supervisor declares the worker's at start.** `verdict.rendered` gains
  an optional strict `tuple` (the five-field tuple of
  `qualification.md`) from `seed/4` (D4). `seed verdict render` takes
  `--principal`, `--model` and `--tool-policy` and fills harness and
  environment from the workspace it verifies in (the local worktree
  adapter's two fields), never inventing the three it cannot know: the
  `run start` posture. A render whose tier requires L2 and which
  declares no tuple refuses at usage, naming the flags; one whose tier
  requires only L1 may omit them and records L1 (or L3 on an executable
  spec). The declared tuple is a declaration, not a report: what the
  verifier ran as is what its operator says, exactly as the worker's
  is, and the qualification machinery that would prove it is item 2's
  evals applied to verifier keys, out of scope here.

- **D3 — enforced at admission, at render and along the merge chain;
  the level is exactly what the records support.** The verdict rule:
  `independence` must be a member of `{L1, L2, L3}` from `seed/4` (`L1`
  alone before it); the claimed level must **equal** the level the
  record supports at the boundary (`LevelAchieved`: L2 by the D1
  comparison of the payload tuple against the window's admitted
  declaration; L3 by an executable, gated acceptance with the
  reproduction half left to recomputation, since the boundary runs
  nothing), never merely be no greater than it (review finding on the
  plan PR: a claim below the computed maximum would underreport the
  audit data D1 exists to produce); and the level must satisfy the
  tier's requirement read through `TierGates`. A claim that is not the
  computed level refuses as a verdict shape refusal naming both; a
  level short of the tier refuses exit 17 `not_independent` with the
  refining code **`level_short`** (`envelope.md`'s refinement table:
  the family's answer, "this verifier cannot judge this contract", is
  already right, and the extra word says it is the level rather than
  the key). `seed verdict render` computes the achieved level from the
  same facts before drafting, so the client never drafts a doomed
  verdict, and writes it into the payload. The fold's `VerdictFact`
  records `Independence` and the verifier's `Tuple`.

  **The merge chain reapplies the tier** (review finding on the plan
  PR). `verdictBoundary`, which `merge.requested`, `merge.observed`,
  `contract.returned` and the red-verdict lockout consult, gains the
  level check beside the grant and the disjointness: a folded verdict
  whose recorded level the record does not support, or which is short
  of the subject's tier, authenticates nothing, so a raw-pushed
  `critical` verdict at `L1` cannot be laundered into `done` through an
  admitted merge chain. Reconcile's class (D5) covers the same two
  conditions.

- **D4 — `seed/4`, and the eval gate becomes a list.** The level
  vocabulary and the verdict's `tuple` field are defined at `seed/4`
  (`version.Seed4` joins `Supported()` and `Activated`; a new
  `version.LevelsApply(v)` is true for `seed/4` exactly, the item 2
  posture for a field a `seed/3` validator's strict verdict decode
  would refuse). `version.EvalApplies` becomes the named list
  `{seed/3, seed/4}`: the equality was right while `seed/3` was the
  newest version and wrong the moment it is not, which
  `memory/LEARNINGS.md` predicted; `Applies("seed/9")` stays false on
  every gate. A `seed/3`-only build refuses an upgraded chain at the
  first `seed/4` record by version, never by judging a level; every
  existing fixture chain verifies byte for byte.

- **D5 — the projections surface what raw pushes claim.** `internal/
  reconcile` gains **`independence_unverified`**: a folded verdict
  whose recorded level the records do not support (L2 with no tuple or
  a same-provider, same-family, same-harness one; L3 on a prose-only
  or ungated spec, or, in the evidence-grade half that may read the
  store and the repository, an L3 whose receipt does not reproduce), or
  which is short of the subject's tier, the `verdict_unverified`
  posture one field over. The report's contracts view shows the level
  beside the verdict.

- **D6 — the fixtures pay for the tier they drive.** The small-team
  and fleet drills gain a `critical` contract: the verifier renders
  declaring a second model family and reaches `done` at L2; the same
  family refuses `level_short`; an executable, gated spec reaches L3
  with one family. `next/lanes/verifier.json`'s summary names the
  declaration.

- **D7 — scope guard.** No rubric, gold set or calibration (item 4); no
  ranking of tuples; no qualification of verifier keys (item 2's evals
  apply to `claim` holders); no change to `internal/eval` beyond the
  gate list; no transition row moves; one refinement code under exit
  17 and one reconcile class; no new exit; no CLI verb beyond the three
  flags on `verdict render`.

## Acceptance Criteria

**Boundary set (new, shown working):**

1. `tiers.md` declares `independence` per tier and the spec-mirror pin
   parses the column, failing in either direction (a planted spec
   value, a planted code row); `TierGates` of an unknown tier requires
   L3.
2. From `seed/4`, `verdict.rendered` admits `independence` in
   `{L1, L2, L3}` and refuses any other literal; at a `seed/3` tip
   `L2`, `L3` and the `tuple` field refuse naming the version, and a
   `seed/3`-only build refuses an upgraded chain at the first `seed/4`
   record by version; every pre-existing fixture chain verifies byte
   for byte.
3. A claimed level that is not the level the record supports refuses
   at admission with nothing appended, each its own row: L2 with no
   tuple; L2 with a tuple equal to the window's declaration; L2
   differing only in principal, tool policy, environment, model version
   or harness version; L3 on a prose-only spec; L3 on an ungated
   executable spec; and L1 claimed where the record supports L2 or L3
   (the underreport). L2 admits on a different model family, on a
   different provider of the same family (three-part models), and,
   separately, on a different harness name; L3 admits on an
   executable, gated spec with the same tuple.
4. A level short of the tier refuses exit 17 with code `level_short`
   naming the tier, the required and the achieved level: L1 on
   `critical`; L2 satisfies `critical`; L3 satisfies every tier; L1
   satisfies `trivial` and `standard`; an unknown tier (raw-pushed)
   requires L3.
5. `seed verdict render` computes the achieved level from the window's
   declaration, its own declared tuple and the folded acceptance, and
   writes it: L1 with no flags on a prose spec, L2 with a different
   family, L3 on an executable gated spec; on a `critical` contract it
   refuses at usage without the three flags and refuses `level_short`
   with the same family on a prose spec; `verdict check` and the
   contracts view render the level.
6. `independence_unverified` is classified for a raw-pushed verdict
   claiming L2 with no tuple or a same-provider same-family
   same-harness one, for L3 on a prose-only spec, for an L3 whose
   receipt does not reproduce (the evidence-grade half), and for a
   verdict short of its subject's tier; never for a supported claim
   that meets the tier. A raw-pushed `critical` verdict at `L1` does
   not authenticate: `merge.requested` citing it refuses and the chain
   cannot reach `done` through it, and the red-verdict lockout ignores
   a tier-short raw fail exactly as it ignores an ungranted one.
7. In small-team mode a `critical` contract reaches `done` with the
   verifier declaring a second model family (L2), and the same family
   refuses `level_short`; in fleet mode an executable gated `critical`
   contract reaches `done` at L3 with one family.
8. **Mutation evidence.** Each must fail a drill: the family comparison
   reading the whole model string; the provider segment ignored; the
   harness comparison dropped; L3 granted on a prose-only spec; the
   admission equality relaxed to "no greater than"; `verdictBoundary`
   skipping the level and tier reapplication; the tier requirement
   read from the constant instead of the column; the order treating L3
   as below L2; `EvalApplies` back to an equality; `LevelsApply` true
   at `seed/3`; the reconcile class skipped for a same-family L2 or a
   tier-short verdict; render writing `L1` unconditionally.
9. `make check` green with coverage measured **cold**, at least three
   readings above the gate, and the suites pass **unprivileged** under
   `setpriv --reuid=65534`.
