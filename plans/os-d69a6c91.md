Implements [`docs/next-build-plan.md`](../docs/next-build-plan.md) Phase 5
item 1: "Transition table as data (`next/spec/transitions.json`),
self-validating, loaded by the admission rule set; lifecycle verbs."
Design authority: [`SEED-NEXT.md`](../SEED-NEXT.md) Part II §6
"Lifecycle" (states `backlog`, `ready`, `in_progress`, `review`, `done`,
`blocked`, `cancelled`; **claim is the ready→in_progress transition,
not a state**; every exit from `in_progress` is deliberate — submit,
release, park, reap — and silent abandonment is impossible by
construction), §8 (done is a verdict **and a reconciliation**:
`verdict.rendered(pass) → merge.requested → merge.observed → done`),
the Appendix verb catalog (the canonical event names), and conformance
III.F: "The lifecycle vocabulary and transition rules are
self-validating data enforced at admission; claim is a transition, not
a state." Deps: Phase 3 (merged) and Phase 4 (stack #109/#111/#112 in
review; implementation stacks on the stack tip until it merges). The
contract is data, not branching: hand-written conditionals that
re-derive what the table says would be a design violation.

## Scope decision (binding for this task)

- **Ships now**: the table, its self-validation, the admission rule
  enforcing it, the canonical lifecycle vocabulary with **capability
  rows for every lifecycle verb**, contract-completeness presence
  checks at the claimability transition, the state fold, and the two
  projection derivations the fold unlocks (contracts `state`; the
  queue's real readiness) — exercising Phase 4's derivation-version
  machinery in anger.
- **Deferred, per the build plan's own items**: claim fences,
  exclusivity, online-only claiming, and contention envelopes (5.2 —
  `claim.taken`'s *transition legality and capability* land now, its
  fence mechanics do not); packets and reap obligations (5.3 —
  `claim.reaped`'s legality lands now, what a reap must leave behind
  does not); the acceptance-spec **content schema, review gate, and
  sealed commitment** (5.4 and Phase 6 — presence is checked now, see
  below); plan-gating (5.5); the verdict pipeline and the observer
  lane (Phase 6 — see the review exits below). III.F's
  transition-table row is met by this task; the fence, packet,
  spec-gate, and verdict rows stay with their items and the exit
  record will say so.
- **Vocabulary comes from the charter's Appendix catalog** (review
  finding on #113 — an earlier draft invented names): `intent.filed`,
  `contract.specified`, `contract.blocked`, `contract.cancelled`,
  `claim.taken`, `claim.released`, `claim.parked`, `claim.reaped`,
  `submission.made`, `merge.observed`. There is **no "promote" verb**:
  the backlog→ready transition *is* `contract.specified` ("acceptance
  spec gate passed"), so claimability and specification are one
  transition by construction. One addition under the catalog's
  explicit `contract.*` ellipsis: `contract.unblocked`
  (`blocked`→`ready`), the inverse the state machine requires, named
  in the spec as a catalog extension.
- **The transition rows (binding)**: `intent.filed` (birth →
  `backlog`), `contract.specified` (`backlog`→`ready`), `claim.taken`
  (`ready`→`in_progress`), `submission.made`
  (`in_progress`→`review`), `claim.released` (`in_progress`→`ready`),
  `claim.parked` (`in_progress`→`blocked`), `claim.reaped`
  (`in_progress`→`ready`), **`merge.observed` (`review`→`done`)** —
  done is reached only through the §8 reconciliation chain's final
  observation, never a bare "accept" (review finding on #113); the
  chain's other events (`verdict.rendered`, `merge.requested`) are
  free stream events in 5.1 and gain their pipeline semantics in
  Phase 6 — `contract.blocked` (`ready`→`blocked`),
  `contract.unblocked` (`blocked`→`ready`), `contract.cancelled`
  ({`backlog`,`ready`,`blocked`,`review`}→`cancelled`).
  `contract.cancelled` deliberately has **no** `in_progress` source:
  leaving `in_progress` happens only through the four deliberate
  exits — silent abandonment made structural. A failed verdict's
  return path out of `review` is Phase 6's to define and is recorded
  as a named extension point, not guessed here; `review` still
  reaches a terminal state via `contract.cancelled`.
- **Contract completeness is enforced at the claimability
  transition** (review finding on #113): the charter's birth rule
  says a contract becomes claimable only when it carries intent
  prose, an acceptance spec, and tier/budget/routing. 5.1 enforces
  **presence** at the shape level: `intent.filed`'s payload must
  carry non-empty `intent`, `tier`, `budget`, and `routing`;
  `contract.specified`'s payload must carry a non-empty `acceptance`
  reference. Content schemas, the review gate for executable
  acceptance, and the sealed commitment are exactly 5.4's and
  Phase 6's items — presence now, gating there, stated in the spec.
- **Every lifecycle verb gets a capability row** (review finding on
  #113 — a verb without a row needs active standing only, which
  would let any enrolled actor cancel or specify anything): two new
  capabilities join the catalog. `dispatch` (queue management):
  `intent.filed`, `contract.specified`, `contract.blocked`,
  `contract.unblocked`, `claim.reaped` accept {`dispatch`,
  `operator`}. `claim` (the worker set): `claim.taken`,
  `claim.released`, `claim.parked`, `submission.made` accept
  {`claim`, `operator`}. `contract.cancelled` and `merge.observed`
  accept {`operator`} only in v0 (Phase 6 adds the observer lane for
  `merge.observed`; cancellation stays operator-gated until a real
  need appears). The normative table in `next/spec/actors.md` gains
  these rows, `keyring.AcceptedCapabilities` mirrors them, and the
  spec-parsing vocabulary test keeps them honest.
- **Lifecycle legality is admission policy**, exactly like halt,
  classification, and grants (the recorded no-protocol-bump stance):
  admission refuses illegal transitions at `seed/1` (seed/0
  grandfathered inert, keyring precedent); verification tolerates
  them in history (the cooperative posture's named consequence), and
  the projection fold skips them **visibly** — an `anomalies` count
  per contract entry, never silent. Exit code: the existing 3
  `invalid_transition` (v1-continuity; semantics match), via a typed
  `InvalidTransitionError{Subject, From, Verb}`; completeness
  refusals are shape refusals; capability refusals are the existing
  14 `out_of_grant`.

## Steps

1. **The table** (`next/spec/transitions.json`): `schema_version`,
   the state list with `initial` and `terminal` markers, and one row
   per verb `{verb, from: [states] | null, to: state}` (`from: null`
   marks the single birth verb).
2. **The loader and fold** (`next/internal/transition`):
   `Load`/`Parse` returning `Table`; **self-validation** at parse
   (each failure named): every `from`/`to` names a declared state;
   exactly one birth verb; terminal states have no outgoing rows; no
   duplicate verb; every state reachable from the initial state;
   every non-terminal state reaches a terminal one (no wedge); the
   `in_progress` outgoing set is exactly the four deliberate exits.
   `Table.Check(current, verb)` → next state or
   `InvalidTransitionError`; `Table.IsLifecycleVerb(verb)`;
   `StateAt(records, subject)` folds a subject's lifecycle events to
   (state, anomalies), skipping illegal history without wedging
   (mirror of `halt.StateAt`'s posture).
3. **The admission rule** (`next/internal/admit`): a `lifecycle` rule
   after `grant`, active at `seed/1`: for a table verb, fold the
   subject's prior lifecycle events and check legality — a birth verb
   on an existing subject refuses, a non-birth verb on an unknown
   subject refuses, an illegal (from, verb) refuses, all as
   `invalid_transition` naming subject, current state, and verb; the
   completeness presence checks for `intent.filed` and
   `contract.specified` refuse at shape. The hook and CLI inherit the
   rule through the shared set; the envelope maps the typed error to
   exit 3.
4. **Capabilities** (`next/internal/keyring`, `next/spec/actors.md`):
   `CapDispatch` and `CapClaim` join the catalog;
   `AcceptedCapabilities` gains the rows above; the normative table
   gains the same rows; the spec-parsing vocabulary test extends
   bidirectionally.
5. **The projections** (`next/internal/project`): the contracts view
   gains `state` and `anomalies` per entry (contracts
   `Version: "2"`); the queue's derivation becomes the table's ready
   set — `derivation: "transitions/1"`, `ready` listing subjects
   whose folded state is `ready` with `since_position` the position
   that made them ready (queue `Version: "2"`) — retiring
   `QueueDerivationNone` exactly as 4.2's spec promised, and proving
   Phase 4's version-bearing republication machinery on a real
   derivation change.
6. **Spec** (`next/spec/lifecycle.md`, new): the state machine (with
   the generated-prose caveat: the docs fan-out that generates
   lifecycle prose from the table is a later phase's machinery; until
   then this file quotes the table and a drill pins their agreement),
   the canonical vocabulary and the one catalog extension, the
   completeness presence rule and where its gate lands, the review
   exits and the named Phase 6 extension point, the capability rows,
   the self-validation invariants, admission-policy activation and
   the visible-anomalies consequence, and the conformance mapping.
   `projections.md` updates the queue and contracts sections;
   `envelope.md` row 3 names lifecycle refusals; `actors.md` gains
   the capability rows (step 4).
7. **Drills** (library + admission + hook + CLI + projections):
   - *Self-validation*: planted bad tables refuse by name — unknown
     state, two birth verbs, terminal with an outgoing row,
     unreachable state, wedged state, an `in_progress` exit set that
     is not exactly the four deliberate exits; the real file loads
     clean and the spec prose agrees with the table (parsed, not
     duplicated).
   - *Legality end-to-end*: the full happy path
     (filed→specified→taken→made→merge.observed) admits through the
     rule set, the seed-admit hook, and the CLI; illegal jumps
     (`claim.taken` on `backlog`, `merge.observed` on `in_progress`,
     birth on an existing subject, non-birth on a fresh subject)
     refuse exit 3 naming subject, state, and verb.
   - *Deliberate exits*: `contract.cancelled` on an `in_progress`
     subject refuses structurally; released/parked/reaped/made all
     admit and land where the table says.
   - *Completeness*: `intent.filed` without tier/budget/routing and
     `contract.specified` without an acceptance reference refuse at
     shape; a specified contract is claimable, an unspecified one is
     not (`claim.taken` on `backlog` refuses).
   - *Capabilities*: a `claim`-granted worker can take, release,
     park, and submit but not specify, reap, or cancel; a
     `dispatch`-granted actor the inverse; an ungranted active actor
     refuses 14 `out_of_grant` on every lifecycle verb; roots pass
     everywhere (implicit operator).
   - *Tolerant fold*: a raw-git chain carrying an illegal transition
     verifies (admission policy, not chain validity), the contracts
     view reports the subject's `anomalies` count, and the folded
     state ignores the illegal event.
   - *Projections*: the queue lists exactly the ready subjects with
     correct `since_position` on populated and root-only fixtures;
     the derivation bump republishes under new build ids at an
     unchanged tip; the contracts `state` field matches the fold.
   - *Vocabulary boundary*: free work verbs (`progress.milestone`,
     and the not-yet-piped `verdict.rendered`/`merge.requested`)
     admit on subjects in every state and appear in contract streams
     untouched.

## Acceptance Criteria

**Boundary set (new, shown working):**

- `next/spec/transitions.json` loads through self-validation; each
  planted invariant violation refuses with a named failure; the
  `in_progress` outgoing set is pinned to the four deliberate exits.
- The full lifecycle happy path admits end-to-end (rule set, hook,
  CLI) with `done` reached only through `merge.observed`; every
  illegal case refuses exit 3 naming subject, current state, and
  verb; `contract.cancelled` from `in_progress` is impossible.
- Incomplete contracts cannot become claimable: the completeness
  presence refusals hold and `claim.taken` on an unspecified subject
  refuses.
- Every lifecycle verb is capability-gated per the actors.md rows
  (drilled positively and negatively); the spec-parsing vocabulary
  test pins code and table together.
- Records under `seed/0` are inert to the rule; at `seed/1` the rule
  is active; an illegal transition in raw-pushed history still
  verifies and surfaces as a visible `anomalies` count.
- The queue publishes `derivation: "transitions/1"` listing exactly
  the ready subjects with correct `since_position`; the contracts
  view carries the folded `state`; both republish under new
  version-bearing build ids at an unchanged tip.

**Retention set (existing, shown unharmed):**
