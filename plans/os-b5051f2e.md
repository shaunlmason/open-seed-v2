# Plan: next — the audit's guardrail bar gains its ceiling arm from the declaration (os-b5051f2e)

`plans/os-16e55c11.md` D5 words III.R row 5's guardrail bar as "no
refusal followed by a blind retry; every claim within its ceiling once
item 4 lands". Neither clause is implemented, and os-aaec6a3c's plan
(D6) says why neither could be folded in there: the ceiling is
admission policy read from the deployment declaration, which the
ledger does not carry, and a refused append never lands. This card
owns the choice the plan named: thread the declaration through the
verb, mirroring admission, or keep the bar ledger-only and correct the
wording. It does both halves honestly: the ceiling arm reads the
declaration when the audit is given one, and the blind-retry clause is
stated as not the chain's to show and routed to the surface that does
show it. Found by review on #312. Tier: standard (it changes what a
conformance bar counts). Deps: none.

## What the tree actually shows

- **Admission's `ceiling` rule is declaration-driven and kind-aware.**
  `policyRules` in `internal/admit` refuses a `claim.taken` signed by
  a key whose roster kind is not `human` on a contract whose tier is
  above the squad's `max_agent`, reading `Context.Declaration`
  (`posture.Config.AgentCeiling`); a ceiling outside the tier
  vocabulary fails closed; no declaration, an undeclared squad, or a
  human key is a no-op. It is policy, not chain validity: a
  raw-pushed claim above the ceiling folds as filed and the chain
  verifies byte for byte.
- **The audit takes records alone.** `simulate.Audit(records)`
  reads the bars from the chain, which is III.R row 5's own contract.
  Its guardrail bar has one arm today, the sealed-author claim
  (os-aaec6a3c), whose facts the fold carries. The kind of a claiming
  key and the tier and routing of its subject are also chain facts:
  `keyring.StateAt` replays the roster from the records and the fold
  carries `Tier` and `Routing` from `intent.filed`. Only the ceiling
  itself lives outside the chain.
- **The remote verbs already read the declaration one way.**
  `deploymentDeclaration` (`cmd/seed/remote.go`): `--config`, else
  `$SEED_CONFIG`, else `./seed.json` when it exists, else none; a
  declaration that exists and does not parse refuses rather than
  falling back. `seed ledger audit` reads none.
- **A blind retry never reaches the chain.** A refused append lands
  nothing, so a refusal followed by a retry leaves only the retry.
  What does see refusals is the client's refusal journal and the
  report's refusal-rate metric (`next/spec/refusals.md`, Phase 8
  item 3), which by design never read the chain.

## Design decisions (binding for this task)

- **D1 — the ceiling arm mirrors admission's rule, under a
  declaration the caller gives.** `simulate.AuditUnder(records,
  declaration)` is the audit with a `*posture.Config`; `Audit(records)`
  is `AuditUnder(records, nil)` and every existing caller keeps its
  meaning. With a declaration, the guardrail bar gains an arm: for
  each `claim.taken`, the claiming key's roster kind is read from the
  keyring replayed over the records to that position (the keyring
  package's own replay, so the roster at the claim is the roster
  admission saw), the subject's tier and routing from the fold, and
  the squad's `max_agent` from the declaration; a non-human key on a
  contract above the ceiling is a breach, a ceiling outside the tier
  vocabulary is a breach (admission fails closed there, so the bar
  does too), and a human key, an undeclared squad or an absent
  guardrails block is silence, exactly as at admission. A chain whose
  roster does not replay yields no kinds to judge and the arm is
  silent, admission's `Keyring == nil` posture; verification already
  refuses such a chain before the audit reads it.
- **D2 — the evidence names itself.** A ceiling breach is appended as
  `<subject>: <kind> key <fingerprint> claimed a <tier> contract at
  position <n> above the <squad> squad's agent ceiling <ceiling>` (or
  `whose agent ceiling <ceiling> is not a tier`), because a bar's list
  is evidence rather than a boolean and a reader must be able to tell
  this arm's finding from the sealed-author arm's.
- **D3 — `seed ledger audit` reads the declaration the way the remote
  verbs do, and says which.** `--config <path>` joins the verb, with
  the fixed lookup (`--config`, `$SEED_CONFIG`, `./seed.json`) shared
  through `deploymentDeclaration`, so an audit and the admission it
  judges read one file by one rule. The result carries `declaration`
  (the path read, or `null`), so a reading is explicit about the
  policy it was judged under even when the file was found implicitly.
  A declaration that exists and does not parse refuses
  `posture_invalid` before any bar, as the remote verbs refuse before
  any transport. The arm judges every claim under the one declaration
  given, which is the caller's assertion about the policy epoch: the
  ledger carries no declaration version or digest, so a deployment
  whose guardrails changed after claims landed audits each epoch with
  the declaration it ran under, and the reading names which it was
  judged under rather than pretending the chain can bind each claim to
  its contemporaneous file. Mirroring admission goes exactly as far as
  admission's own inputs go (review on #322).
- **D4 — the blind-retry clause is explicitly unmet, not routed.** A
  refused append never lands, so no bar over the chain can see a
  refusal, let alone a retry of it; and the refusal journal
  (`refusals.md`) cannot establish it either as it stands, since an
  entry keeps actor, verb, subject, outcome, code and position but no
  digest of the attempted payload, so an unchanged retry and a
  corrected one write indistinguishable entries and the report
  aggregates them into counts (review on #322). No surface can measure
  the clause today. `simulation.md`'s bar 4 says so in those words,
  names the clause unmet rather than covered, and cites the card that
  owns the measurement, os-a9e715dc (the journal entry gains the
  attempted record's digest and the report counts a same-digest retry
  as blind). The charter's row is untouched (the conformance table
  holds it row for row); what is corrected is the bar's own
  description, and it is corrected toward less coverage, not more.
- **D5 — the simulation declares a ceiling and audits under it.** The
  seven-day conformance run is the audit that is supposed to show every
  claim stayed within its ceiling, so an arm silent there would be a
  claim nobody measured (review on #322). The throwaway deployment
  writes a `seed.json` beside its remote (the run's posture, the core
  squad's agents ceilinged at `trivial`, the tier the catalog files
  at, the shipped lanes as the team), every verb that takes `--config`
  reads it (the loop verbs, `ledger append`, `verdict render`, the
  merge verbs, `knowledge propose`), so the ceiling rule is live at
  every claim the run admits, and the end-of-run audit is
  `AuditUnder(records, that declaration)`. `AuditResult` gains
  `declared`, whether the ceiling arm judged under a declaration with
  guardrails, so a clean reading says whether the ceiling was among
  the things it read; the simulate command renders it and the
  simulate drills assert it. `maintain run` takes no `--config` and is
  unchanged: the maintenance pass claims nothing.
- **D6 — the cost is measured.** One keyring replay over the chain
  and one fold, then a lookup per claim; the drill audits an
  admission-grade chain from `internal/history` under a declaration
  and the PR records the time, as os-88df7ab2 did.
- **D7 — bounds.** NOT `internal/admit/**`, NOT
  `internal/transition/**`, NOT `internal/keyring/**`, NOT
  `internal/posture/**`, NOT `internal/history/**`, NOT the other four
  bars, NOT `AuditedVerbs`. The verb's exit codes and the shape of
  its evidence lists are unchanged; the envelope gains one field.

## Acceptance Criteria

**Boundary set (new, shown working):**

1. **The arm mirrors admission.** Under a declaration, a raw
   `claim.taken` by an agent-kind key on a contract above its squad's
   `max_agent` is a guardrail breach naming the subject, kind, tier,
   position, squad and ceiling; a human key, an undeclared squad, a
   claim at the ceiling and an absent declaration are silence; a
   ceiling outside the vocabulary is a breach.

   `internal/history` audits with `guardrail_breaches` empty under a
   declaration whose ceiling covers its tiers, and the cost of the
   reading is recorded in the PR.

   `seed ledger audit --config` refuses `audit_violated` naming the
   ceiling breach where the same chain without a declaration does
   not; the result names the declaration read or `null`; `$SEED_CONFIG`
   is honored; a malformed declaration refuses `posture_invalid` before
   any bar.

   bar 4 says no surface can measure the clause today, why the journal
   cannot as it stands, and names the card that owns the measurement.
5. **The simulation exercises the arm.** `seed simulate` under either
   posture admits every claim under a declared ceiling and its audit
   reports `declared: true` and clean.
