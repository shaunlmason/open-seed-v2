# Plan: next — Phase 10 item 4, rubric verdicts (per-item, evidence-cited, uncertainty-marked); calibration harness with authority suspension on drift (os-2e34f66a)

The build plan's Phase 10 item 4: *"Rubric verdicts (per-item,
evidence-cited, uncertainty-marked); calibration harness against a gold
set with authority suspension on drift."* The charter's definition
(§7, "Qualitative residue"): *"Acceptance that cannot be a command
(tone, judgment, taste) is a rubric the verifier scores item-by-item
with cited evidence and explicit uncertainty — never a single holistic
score; low-confidence items route to human verdict; rubric calibration
runs against a human-scored gold set with automatic authority
suspension on drift (§16)."* §16: *"Verifier calibration: scheduled
sampling against a human gold set; automatic authority suspension on
drift; defect contract filed."* Conformance III.G row 7 and III.O row
2 are the targets.

This plan is written against the evals of os-03e47abb (#221), the tier
table of os-be12ac16 (#222) and the levels of os-99829835 (#223, the
verifier's declared tuple at render and the `seed/4` register entry),
and depends on all three merging first.

## What the tree actually shows

Measured, not assumed:

- **A verdict on a prose-only spec is one word the verifier declares.**
  `seed verdict render --verdict pass|fail` on a spec with no
  executable content leaves the judgment entirely to the flag
  (`verdicts.md`, "Rendering derives from the transcripts"): no items,
  no evidence, no uncertainty, exactly the holistic score the charter
  forbids. `plan.Commands` reads one section of the acceptance spec,
  "Validation commands", and nothing else; the spec is a gated,
  commit-anchored artifact (`acceptance.md`).
- **Payloads carry references, never bodies.** The classification lint
  bounds free text at admission; receipts, transcripts and every bulk
  artifact live in the content-addressed store cited by digest
  (`protocol.md`, "Data classification"). A scorecard is bulk.
- **The human gate is the escalation, and it freezes.**
  `escalation.raised` from `review` moves the subject to `blocked`;
  `decision.recorded` returns it to `ready`, never to `review`, so a
  verdict cannot follow a decision on the same submission
  (`escalation.md`, `lifecycle.md`). A human verdict needs a path that
  leaves the subject in `review`.
- **`verdict.rendered` accepts `verdict` and nothing else**, with no
  operator fallback by design; a human who renders holds a `verdict`
  grant on their own key. The tier table's `human review` column
  (`tiers.md`) is declared and consumed by nobody.
- **Evals already turn a known verdict into a grant or its
  withdrawal**, for `claim` holders and their runtime tuples
  (`evals.md`): a definition under `next/evals/<name>/`, filed as an
  ordinary contract, judged through the production verbs, the pass
  minting `actor.qualified` and the fail `actor.disqualified`, with
  spot-checks aging from the record. The qualification verbs refuse
  any capability but `claim` (review finding on #221). Item 3 gives
  the verifier a declared tuple at render.
- **The maintenance loop files defect contracts from a closed lint
  set**, idempotently through the ledger (`maintenance.md`), and the
  eval derivation reports what it owes by lane (`seed eval status`).

## Design decisions (binding for this task)

- **D1 — the rubric is a section of the acceptance spec, parsed like
  the commands.** A `## Rubric` section whose bullets are items
  `- <id>: <criterion>` (`id` a slug unique within the spec) is the
  residue: what the spec could not make a command. `plan.Rubric(doc)
  []Item` reads it exactly as `plan.Commands` reads its section; a
  spec may carry both, and gate-before-run covers the rubric as it
  covers the commands (the verifier acts on gated content only). A
  spec with a rubric and `executable: false` is the prose-only case
  made structured; a rubric with a duplicate or empty id refuses at
  render as `spec_unrunnable`.

- **D2 — the scorecard is an artifact the verdict cites; the
  derivation-bearing half of it travels in the signed payload.** The
  verifier's scoring is one JCS-canonical scorecard in the artifact
  store: `{"contract", "submission", "items": [{"id", "score":
  "pass"|"fail", "evidence": ["<path> @ <commit>#L<a>-L<b>" |
  "transcript:<n>" ...], "uncertainty": "low"|"high", "note"}]}`, and
  `verdict.rendered` gains optional **`scorecard`** from `seed/4`, the
  register entry item 3 creates: `{"digest", "items": [{"id",
  "score", "uncertainty"}]}`, the artifact's digest and, per item,
  exactly the two enums the derivation reads. Evidence and notes are
  bulk and stay in the artifact; ids and two enums are coordination
  facts the classification budget admits, and putting them on the
  record is what lets every boundary that consumes a verdict reapply
  the derivation from the record alone, since the admission context
  carries no artifact store by design (`admit.go`'s checkpoint rule
  says so in as many words). Every rubric item is scored exactly once,
  an unknown id refuses, every item cites at least one evidence
  reference (an anchored path or a transcript the receipt carries,
  never prose), `note` is bounded by the classification budget, and
  `uncertainty` is the verifier's explicit confidence with two values,
  because the charter asks for explicit uncertainty and a routing
  decision, and three values would invite the middle. Never a holistic
  score: the payload carries the digest and the per-item outcome, the
  artifact carries the evidence.

- **D3 — render derives the verdict from the scorecard exactly as it
  derives from the transcripts.** With a rubric present, `seed verdict
  render` requires `--scorecard <file>`, validates it (D2), stores it,
  and derives the permissible verdict: `pass` requires every item
  `pass` at `low` uncertainty, a `fail` item forbids `pass` (the
  `checks_red` family, refining code **`rubric_red`**), and an item at
  `high` uncertainty forbids BOTH verdicts, refining code
  **`human_verdict`**: low confidence routes to a human, and the render
  says so. The admission rule reapplies the same derivation to the
  payload's items: a `pass` whose own items carry a `fail` or a `high`
  refuses, as does a render on a rubric spec that cites no scorecard,
  and **`verdictBoundary` reapplies it too**, beside the grant, the
  disjointness and item 3's level, so a folded verdict that fails its
  own derivation authenticates nothing: `merge.requested` and
  `merge.observed` citing it refuse, `contract.returned` cannot cite
  it, and the red-verdict lockout does not count it. That is the
  record-derivable half, checked at every boundary with no store. The
  artifact-derivable half is `verdict check`'s and reconcile's, exactly
  `receipt_mismatch`'s posture: a cited scorecard that does not
  retrieve, or whose items disagree with the payload's, refuses in
  `verdict check` and classifies **`scorecard_unverified`** in
  reconcile. The residual is stated: a verifier that signs a
  self-consistent false record (all items `pass` in the payload, a red
  artifact in the store) is the verifier's lie, which L1, D5's
  calibration and reconcile answer, as they answer a false pass over a
  red receipt today (review finding on #225).

- **D4 — the human verdict is a deferral fact and an operator-standing
  verifier key, never an escalation.** `verdict.deferred` (catalog
  growth under `verdict.*`, additive) is a fact admitted in `review`
  by a `verdict` key, changing no state, carrying `{"scorecard",
  "submission", "items": [<ids at high uncertainty>]}`. It creates the
  obligation **`verdict.human`** owed by the operator lane
  ([`obligations.md`](../next/spec/obligations.md)), and **a human is a key with
  operator standing**: the tree's one structural proxy for a person,
  the standing `decision.recorded` demands and enrollment `kind`
  explicitly is not (`actors.md`: `kind` is an assertion by the
  enrolling operator and supports no security-relevant decision).
  After a deferral, and on a tier with `human review: yes` from the
  first render, `verdict.rendered` on that submission admits only from
  a signer that holds an explicit `verdict` grant AND operator standing
  at the position (a governance root's implicit standing or an
  explicit `operator` grant), under the L1 rule like any verifier; a
  `verdict`-only key, the deferring one included, refuses
  `human_verdict`. `verdict.rendered`'s accepted set stays `verdict`
  alone: operator standing is a second requirement on these
  submissions, never a fallback, so III.G's "no disguised verdict"
  holds. `verdictBoundary` reapplies the requirement from the record,
  so a raw-pushed machine pass on a deferred or human-review
  submission authenticates nothing for the merge chain. `seed verdict
  defer --scorecard` appends the deferral; `seed situation` surfaces
  the debt. The tier table's `human review` column gets its consumer:
  on such a tier a render without operator standing is refused
  `human_verdict` whatever the scorecard says, and the deferral is the
  only machine act, which is the charter's "humans review only
  high-tier work" made structural.

  Refused: routing through `escalation.raised`. An escalation freezes
  the contract and its answer returns the subject to `ready`, so the
  verdict could never follow the decision on the submission it
  judged; a deferral leaves the subject in `review` for the human's
  render.

- **D5 — calibration is an eval for verifier keys, with a gold
  scorecard as the known verdict, committed to in the tree and held
  outside it.** A definition under `next/evals/<name>/` with `"kind":
  "calibration"` carries a rubric spec in `fixture/`, a `solution/`,
  and **`"calibration": {"gold": "sha256:<digest>"}`**, the digest of
  the human-scored gold scorecard (`{"items": [{"id", "score"}]}`,
  JCS-canonical). The gold itself is **never in the repository**: the
  verifier under calibration works in a clone of the whole tree, so a
  checked-in gold set would be readable from its workspace and a
  drifting verifier could copy it and always meet the floor. The
  operator lane holds the gold outside the tree and supplies it to the
  derivation (`seed eval status|act --gold <dir>`, one `<name>.json`
  per calibration definition); the derivation refuses to score against
  a gold whose canonical digest is not the definition's commitment
  (`gold_mismatch`) and, with none supplied, owes nothing for that
  contract and notes `gold_missing`. Filed with `seed eval file` and
  worked like any eval (the solution applied, the submission made), it
  is judged by the verifier under calibration: the verifier renders
  with its scorecard, and `eval.Due` compares the payload's items to
  the gold item by item. **Agreement** is the fraction of items where
  the verifier's `score` equals the gold's at `low` uncertainty; **the
  floor is policy on the protected spec surface, never a runtime
  argument**: `evals.md` states it (`0.8`), `eval.CalibrationFloor`
  mirrors it, a drill pins the two as `tierTable` is pinned to
  `tiers.md`, and a definition may raise it (`"calibration":
  {"floor"}`) but never lower it (a definition below the spec floor
  refuses at `Load`). Nothing an invoker of `seed eval act` passes can
  move it. At or above the floor, `Due` owes `actor.qualified` for
  capability **`verdict`** and
  the verifier's declared tuple (item 3's); below it, **drift**: `Due`
  owes `actor.disqualified` for `verdict` on that tuple, tuple-wide,
  and a defect filing by the dispatcher naming the contract and the
  disagreeing items (the maintenance loop's idempotent filing shape,
  through `seed eval act`). The qualification verbs widen from `claim`
  alone to `{claim, verdict}`, and **the set rule applies to
  `verdict.rendered`'s declared tuple** exactly as to `run.started`'s:
  an empty never-cited set is the bridge, an empty once-cited set
  admits nothing. That is "automatic authority suspension on drift":
  the verifier's renders under the drifted configuration refuse
  `out_of_grant` until a calibration eval passes again. Spot-checks
  age calibration qualifications exactly as item 2 ages claim ones,
  which is III.O's "runs scheduled".

  Refused: `actor.suspended` from a machine lane. Suspension ends a
  key's standing across every contract, is operator-only by the
  capability table, and is not what the charter asks for; withdrawing
  the `verdict` set for one configuration is exactly the shape item 2
  built, and it re-qualifies by the same road.

- **D6 — versioning.** The `scorecard` field on `verdict.rendered`,
  the `verdict.deferred` verb and the widened qualification capability
  activate at `seed/4` with item 3's fields; a `seed/3` validator
  refuses each at its position by version, and every existing chain
  verifies byte for byte. If #223 lands first this card adds to its
  register entry; if this card lands first it creates the entry and
  #223 adds to it.

- **D7 — scope guard.** No rubric authoring surface; the gold set is
  committed to by digest and held outside the tree, and no gold
  scorecard is checked in anywhere, the drills' included (each drill
  builds its definition in a temporary repository and keeps the gold
  in a directory outside it). **No shipped calibration definition**: a
  definition whose gold the tree carries would be exactly the leak D5
  refuses, and one whose gold the implementer discards is unusable;
  `evals.md` records how a deployment ships one (commit the definition
  with the digest, hold the gold). No model call anywhere: the
  scorecard is the verifier's input, produced by whatever the verifier
  is. The reconciliation chain changes only in `verdictBoundary`'s
  reapplication (D3, D4), no transition row moves (`verdict.deferred`
  changes no state), two refinement codes and no new exit, no change
  to the escalation channel, no change to `seed maintain run`, no CLI
  flag that moves a threshold.

## Acceptance Criteria

**Boundary set (new, shown working):**

1. `plan.Rubric` reads the `## Rubric` section's items with ids and
   criteria; a duplicate or empty id refuses `spec_unrunnable` at
   render; a spec with both sections yields both commands and items.
2. `seed verdict render` on a spec with a rubric refuses at usage
   without `--scorecard`; a scorecard missing an item, scoring an
   unknown one, scoring one twice, citing no evidence, citing evidence
   that resolves to neither an anchored path nor a transcript, or
   carrying a note over the classification budget refuses naming the
   part; a valid scorecard is stored and the verdict cites its digest
   and its items' scores and uncertainties, nothing else of it.
3. The derivation: all items `pass` at `low` renders `pass`; any item
   `fail` refuses `pass` with code `rubric_red` naming the item and
   leaves `fail` renderable; any item at `high` refuses both with code
   `human_verdict`; on a `human review: yes` tier a render from a key
   without operator standing refuses `human_verdict` whatever the
   scorecard says, and one from a key holding `verdict` and operator
   standing admits.
4. `seed verdict defer --scorecard` appends `verdict.deferred` in
   `review` from a `verdict` key not in the implementing set, changes
   no state, and `seed situation` shows `verdict.human` owed by the
   operator lane; a key holding `verdict` and operator standing then
   renders on the same submission citing its own scorecard and the
   debt clears; a `verdict`-only key, the deferring one included,
   refuses `human_verdict` on that submission at admission and, raw-
   pushed, authenticates nothing for `merge.requested`; a second
   deferral on the same submission refuses.
5. A `pass` whose payload items carry a `fail` or a `high` refuses at
   admission (nothing appended); raw-pushed, `merge.requested` citing
   it refuses, `merge.observed` cannot reach `done` through it, the
   lockout ignores a raw fail whose items are all `pass`, and reconcile
   classifies nothing for it (the record already refutes it); a folded
   pass whose payload items are all `pass` at `low` over a stored
   scorecard that disagrees, or that does not retrieve, refuses in
   `verdict check` and classifies `scorecard_unverified`; at a
   `seed/3` tip `scorecard`,
   `verdict.deferred` and a `verdict`-capability qualification each
   refuse naming the version; a `seed/3`-only build refuses an upgraded
   chain at the first `seed/4` record by version.
6. A calibration eval built in a temporary repository with its gold
   held outside it: `seed eval check` proves the fixture red and the
   solution green; `eval.CalibrationFloor` equals the figure
   `evals.md` states, a definition declaring a lower floor refuses at
   `Load` and one declaring a higher floor is scored against it; with
   no `--gold` the contract owes nothing and notes `gold_missing`,
   with a gold whose digest is not the commitment `gold_mismatch` and
   nothing performed; filed and worked, the verifier's scorecard
   agreeing with the gold at or above the floor owes and mints
   `actor.qualified` for `verdict` on the verifier's declared tuple;
   below the floor owes and performs the tuple-wide
   `actor.disqualified` and the dispatcher's defect filing naming the
   disagreeing items; afterwards a render under that tuple refuses
   `out_of_grant` on an ordinary contract while a calibration eval
   still admits; a passing calibration re-qualifies; the qualification
   ages and a spot-check files at the interval; `seed eval list` in
   the shipped tree shows no calibration definition.
7. In small-team mode a rubric contract reaches `done` through a
   scorecard render, a deferred one reaches `done` through the human
   render, and a drifted verifier is refused until re-calibrated.
8. **Mutation evidence.** Each must fail a drill: the rubric parser
   reading the commands section; the scorecard validation skipping the
   evidence check; the derivation letting a `fail` item pass; `high`
   uncertainty treated as `low`; the human-review column ignored; the
   operator-standing requirement dropped after a deferral; the
   deferral admitted from an implementing key; `verdictBoundary`
   skipping the derivation or the standing; `verdict check` skipping
   the payload-versus-artifact comparison; agreement counting `high`
   items as agreeing; the floor compared with `<` instead of `>=`;
   the floor read from anywhere but the spec-pinned constant or a
   definition that raises it; a definition allowed to lower the floor;
   the gold digest not compared to the commitment; the
   disqualification touching only the drifted verifier; the set rule
   skipped at render; the defect filing skipped on drift.
9. `make check` green with coverage measured **cold**, at least three
   readings above the gate, and the suites pass **unprivileged** under
   `setpriv --reuid=65534`.
