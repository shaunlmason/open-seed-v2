## Design decisions (binding for this task)

1. **`check.sealed` is the commitment fact.** Strict payload
   `{"commitment": "<64-hex>"}`; a fact, not a transition; admits only
   while the subject is in `ready` **and the fold shows no prior
   `claim.taken` on the subject** — a subject back in `ready` after
   `claim.released` or `claim.reaped` refuses by name, because
   implementation already began and a commitment appended then proves
   nothing (review finding on this plan: the state check alone would
   admit post-implementation seals through the release path). With
   both halves, the admitted position ordering *is* the proof that
   the checks predate implementation. One commitment per subject: a
   second `check.sealed` refuses (changing sealed checks pre-claim
   means cancel and re-file; rotation re-encrypts, never re-commits).
   Raw-pushed extras stay anomalies under the tolerant fold, the
   established posture.
2. **The commitment is a salted hash of the canonical plaintext.**
   The sealed envelope is `{"salt": "<64-hex>", "checks":
   ["<command>", …]}`; the commitment is SHA-256 over its JCS
   canonical bytes. The 32-byte random salt lives *only inside the
   ciphertext* — publishing it would invite dictionary attacks on
   low-entropy check bodies — so the commitment is verifiable exactly
   by the parties who can decrypt, and verified at every unseal.
3. **Confidentiality rides `filippo.io/age` v1.2.1** (the plan's
   binding default). Recipients are every key holding the `verdict`
   capability at seal or rotate time, wrapped as ssh-ed25519 age
   recipients derived from the enrolled ed25519 public keys
   (`agessh`); verifier private keys decrypt as the matching
   identities. No new key material is enrolled — "recipients = the
   verifier keyring" literally. The spec notes the cross-protocol use
   (one ed25519 key signs verdicts and unwraps seals) and names
   dedicated X25519 enrollment as the successor if the boundary ever
   needs it.
4. **Mutable ciphertext, immutable commitment.** Ciphertext lives at
   `next/var/artifacts/sealed/<commitment>.age` — a named,
   overwritable bucket beside the content-addressed store, referenced
   from the ledger only by the commitment hash. Rotation overwrites
   the file and touches no history; deleting it is the charter's
   erasure path, and the audit surfaces the absence, never silence.
5. **Authoring isolation is enforced twice and audited once.** A new
   `sealer` capability authors seals, and `check.sealed` accepts
   `[sealer]` **only** — no operator fallback (review finding on this
   plan: `operator` already stands in the claim and submission lanes,
   so an operator row here would put authoring and implementation
   authority on one capability and the audit could prove nothing;
   the sealer lane mirrors the verdict lane's deliberate
   no-fallback posture from 6.1). Grant-level disjointness: granting
   `sealer` to a key holding `claim` *or* `operator` refuses, and
   granting `claim` or `operator` to a key holding `sealer` refuses —
   the L1-family posture at the keyring boundary. Per-subject:
   `claim.taken` refuses when the claiming key authored that
   subject's seal. The capability audit test then proves the
   cryptographic half: no implementer identity decrypts.
6. **The verifier unseals and runs.** When the fold carries a
   commitment, `seed verdict render` loads the ciphertext, decrypts
   with `--key` as an age identity, recomputes the commitment from
   the plaintext (mismatch refuses), runs the sealed checks through
   the 6.1 runner profile, and the receipt gains `"commitment"` and
   `"sealed_transcripts"` (the charter's "visible and sealed check
   transcripts"). A pass still derives from transcripts: a red sealed
   check is `checks_red` (exit 20). New refusals: exit 22
   `seal_broken` (ciphertext missing or commitment mismatch), exit 23
   `not_recipient` (the identity cannot decrypt — rotation lag; the
   refusal names `seal rotate`), exit 24 `unsealed` (an above-trivial
   subject with no commitment at render — the "contracts carry sealed
   checks" gate enforced at the verifier boundary; the trivial tier
   is exempt, and every existing render fixture is trivial-tier).
   `seed verdict check` on a sealed subject holds the full
   recompute-and-mismatch guarantee (review finding on this plan:
   presence-and-equality checking would let a raw-pushed receipt
   with invented passing sealed transcripts check green): it
   requires a `--key` able to unseal, decrypts, verifies the
   commitment, reruns the sealed commands, and includes the sealed
   transcripts in the recomputed-receipt comparison — forged sealed
   transcripts are exit 21, a key that cannot decrypt is exit 23,
   and there is no silent partial verification of a sealed subject.
7. **`seed seal` is the authoring and custody surface.**
   `seal create --ledger --repo --subject --checks <file> --key
   <sealer>`: read one command per line (blank and `#` lines
   skipped), refuse unless parsing yields at least one command — an
   empty seal would mark the contract sealed while running zero
   secret checks, vacuously passing the above-trivial gate (review
   finding on this plan) — then salt, canonicalize, commit, encrypt
   to the current verifier keyring, write the ciphertext, append
   `check.sealed`. Render closes the raw-crafted half of the same
   hole: a decrypted envelope with zero checks is exit 22
   `seal_broken`, never a vacuous pass.
   `seal rotate --ledger --repo --key <identity>`: for every
   non-terminal sealed subject, decrypt with the supplied identity,
   re-encrypt to the *current* verifier keyring, overwrite; terminal
   subjects are skipped (exposure bounded to contracts open during a
   compromise window); reports rotated/skipped/failed; writes no
   ledger events. `seal audit --ledger --repo`: detection at exit 0 —
   per non-terminal sealed subject, ciphertext present
   (`seal_evidence_missing`), and the age header's ssh-ed25519
   recipient tags match the current verifier keyring in both
   directions (`recipients_stale` when a current verifier is missing,
   naming rotate; `recipient_foreign` when a stanza matches no
   current verifier). The header scan reads only the documented age
   v1 recipient stanza lines; payloads stay opaque.
8. **Projections surface the seal.** The fold gains
   `Sealed *SealedFact{Pos, Commitment}`; contracts bump to v7 with
   `sealed: {position, commitment} | null` (explicit null, the 6.2
   convention); `internal/reconcile` gains the record-derivable class
   `unsealed` (an above-trivial subject at or past `in_progress` with
   no commitment — reported neutrally; the hard gate is decision 6's
   render refusal); report v4; cache generation 6 / version "7" with
   the sealed columns and the established republish drills.
9. **Honest scope is normative text.** `next/spec/sealed-checks.md`
   states the subsystem is defense-in-depth against specification
   gaming, not a structural solution, and names the load-bearing
   defenses beside it (independent evidence, invariant and
   property-based tests, adversarial cases, human review) — the
   conformance row asks for exactly this sentence family.

## Steps

1. **Spec first.** Write `next/spec/sealed-checks.md` (subsystem,
   commitment shape, recipients and the cross-protocol note, the
   mutable-ciphertext bucket and erasure path, rotation, authoring
   isolation, the render gate and new exits, the audit classes, the
   honest-scope paragraph, conformance mapping); update `actors.md`
   (sealer row, verdict-row cross-reference), `lifecycle.md`
   (`check.sealed` fact and its window), `verdicts.md` (unseal-and-run,
   receipt fields, exits 22–24).
2. **Keyring.** `CapSealer`; the `check.sealed` accepted-capability
   row; grant-level sealer/claim disjointness refusals at
   `actor.granted` admission; a read-only enumerator returning the
   entries holding a capability (rotate and seal need the verifier
   set). Spec-parsing test keeps code and table pinned.
3. **Fold + admission.** `internal/transition`: `SealedFact` captured
   from admitted `check.sealed`; `internal/admit`: the seal rule
   (state `ready`, strict shape, 64-hex commitment, singular per
   subject, capability lane) and the `claim.taken` seal-author
   refusal. Drills: admits in ready with no prior claim; refuses in
   backlog, in_progress, review, and — the release-path drill — in
   ready again after `claim.released` or `claim.reaped`; refuses a
   second seal; refuses bad shapes and short commitments; sealer
   admits, and operator, plain enrolled, and claim keys all refuse
   (no fallback lane); the seal author's later claim refuses; the
   sealer/claim and sealer/operator grant pairs refuse in both
   directions; raw-pushed seals fold tolerated with anomalies
   counted.
4. **Crypto seam.** `internal/seal` (new): envelope canonicalization
   and commitment; encrypt-to-recipients and decrypt-with-identity
   over `agessh`; the named ciphertext bucket in `internal/artifact`
   (validated commitment name, overwrite allowed); the age-header
   recipient-tag scan. Unit drills: commitment round-trip and
   mismatch; encrypt/decrypt round-trip; a non-recipient identity
   fails; rotation changes recipients without changing the
   commitment; tag scan matches the keyring both directions.
5. **CLI.** `seed seal create|rotate|audit` per decision 7 with
   end-to-end drills: create appends the commitment and stores
   ciphertext; create refuses a checks file that parses to zero
   commands; a sealed subject renders with sealed transcripts in
   the receipt and `commitment` set; a red sealed check is exit 20;
   missing ciphertext, a tampered stored body, and a raw-crafted
   empty-checks envelope are exit 22; a non-recipient render key is
   exit 23; an above-trivial unsealed subject refuses render at
   exit 24 while a trivial one renders as today; `verdict check` on
   a sealed subject with a verifier identity reruns the sealed
   commands and catches a stored receipt whose sealed transcripts
   were forged (exit 21), and refuses a non-recipient key at exit
   23; rotate after a verifier grant change re-encrypts (old
   sole-verifier identity stops decrypting when revoked and replaced,
   the new one succeeds, commitment unchanged); audit reports clean
   after rotate, `recipients_stale` before it, `seal_evidence_missing`
   after deletion, `recipient_foreign` for a stanza outside the
   keyring.
6. **The capability audit.** The conformance drill: a history with
   sealer, implementer (claim), and verifier keys; the implementer
   identity fails to decrypt at the age layer; the verifier identity
   succeeds; the sealer's claim refuses; the grant-disjointness
   refusals hold; and no envelope or error path emits the plaintext
   or salt.
7. **Projections.** Contracts v7 (`sealed` explicit-null), reconcile
   `unsealed` class, report v4, cache generation 6 / version "7";
   republish-at-unchanged-tip and byte-identity drills extended.
8. **Docs.** progress.md ledger row and frontier; decisions.md
   (commitment shape, agessh recipients, named bucket, render gate,
   disjointness posture); LEARNINGS/DEADENDS as earned.

## Acceptance Criteria

**Boundary set (new, shown working):**

- The commitment predates implementation by construction:
  `check.sealed` admits only in `ready` with no prior `claim.taken`,
  refuses everywhere else including ready-after-release, refuses a
  second commitment, and the fold carries `{position, commitment}`.
- Authoring isolation holds at every layer drilled: `check.sealed`
  is sealer-only (operator refuses), the sealer/claim and
  sealer/operator grant refusals hold both directions, the seal
  author's claim refuses, and the capability audit proves the
  implementer identity cannot decrypt while the verifier identity
  can.
- The verifier boundary runs sealed checks: render on a sealed
  subject verifies the commitment, runs the sealed commands under
  the 6.1 profile, and lands `commitment` plus `sealed_transcripts`
  in the receipt; red sealed checks are exit 20; exits 22/23/24
  refuse by name for broken seals (an empty-checks envelope
  included), non-recipients, and above-trivial unsealed subjects;
  an empty parsed checks file never commits; `verdict check` reruns
  sealed commands under a verifier identity, so forged sealed
  transcripts in a stored receipt are exit 21, never a green check.
- Rotation re-encrypts without touching history: the ledger is
  byte-identical across a rotate, the commitment unchanged, the
  revoked identity locked out, the current keyring able to decrypt;
  the audit distinguishes clean, stale, missing, and foreign.
- Contracts v7 / report v4 / cache generation 6 carry the sealed
  surface with explicit null and the republish drills green;
  `unsealed` surfaces neutrally for above-trivial gaps.

**Retention set (existing, shown unharmed):**

- Every pre-existing suite is green: `make check` passes at the
  coverage gate; no existing test is weakened, skipped, or deleted.
- Trivial-tier render, check, receipt, reconcile, and the 6.2 chain
  behave exactly as landed (exits 17–21 unchanged); the only new
  refusals are on surfaces that did not exist or were unreachable.
- No v1 surface changes; the task PR never touches `plans/**`.
